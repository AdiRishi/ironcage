A transaction history records money moving. It rarely explains how those movements fit together or what they mean over time. Bills arrive at different intervals. Purchases span several accounts. A large expense dominates one month while a small change in routine becomes a lasting cost.

Ironcage gives one person a connected understanding of their finances. It brings financial history and personal context together so decisions reflect the life behind the records.

## Understanding change

The central questions concern change. Which costs increased? Which habits evolved? Did ordinary life become more expensive, or did an unusual event distort the comparison?

Those questions need context. A mortgage changes ongoing commitments. A trip explains an unusual period. Moving purchases to a credit card changes where payments appear without necessarily changing spending habits.

Financial understanding develops as the history grows and the user explains what the records cannot establish. A useful explanation remains available for the next question.

## Decisions that reflect priorities

Savings matter, but the value of a purchase is personal. Spending can support relationships, interests, health, and comfort. Understanding its cost gives the user room to choose what to change and what to protect.

Ironcage helps the user examine alternatives and understand whether a deliberate change had the intended effect. Its purpose is informed choice, not a judgment that less spending is always better.

## Trust through evidence

A financial explanation is useful when its numbers are consistent, its evidence is available, and its uncertainty is clear. The user needs to understand how a conclusion was reached and what would change it.

Ironcage keeps that connection between an answer and its basis. When records are incomplete or several explanations remain possible, those limits are part of the answer.

## A broader financial life

Spend management establishes an understanding of costs, commitments, and available resources. The longer-term vision includes investments, stock trading, crypto, and other financial decisions, informed by the same personal context and traceable evidence.

Success means the user can answer a financial question, understand why the answer changed, and decide what to do with that knowledge.
