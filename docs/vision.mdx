A bank history records money moving. It does not say who was paid, what the money was for, or why this month cost more than the last. Ironcage reads that history and explains it.

## What it should feel like

Opening Ironcage should feel like sitting with someone who has already read every statement. It knows that the $1,840 on the 3rd went to the landlord and is rent. It knows that Kmart is general shopping and Uniqlo is clothing. It can say that August cost $400 more than July because of three restaurant dinners and a flight. When it isn't sure, it asks one clear question and remembers the answer.

## Understanding every dollar

Every dollar has a source and a destination. Money comes in as salary, interest, refunds, or repayments from friends. It goes out as spending in a category, as a loan repayment, into savings, or toward investments. Moving money between your own accounts is neither. Ironcage shows the whole flow, not only spending.

Spending breaks down as far as the evidence allows: food, then groceries, dining out, and delivery; shopping, then clothing, electronics, and the home. The level of detail comes from knowing who each counterparty is. A mixed retailer such as Amazon or Kmart stays in general shopping, because a bank record does not say what was in the basket.

## AI interprets, code counts

A model does the interpretation work: it identifies counterparties, assigns categories, and decides which movements are spending. It applies what it is confident about and asks about the rest. Every assignment records who made it and why, so a number can say how much of it rests on the model's judgment.

Deterministic shared code computes every total. The model explains numbers that the code produced. It never adds anything up on its own.

## Trust through evidence

A number is worth something when it agrees with every other number, its records are a click away, and its limits are stated. Missing statements are shown as missing, never as zero. A correction you make always wins over the model and over rules, and it keeps working on future imports.

## Decisions that reflect priorities

Knowing what something costs gives you room to decide what to change and what to protect. Ironcage describes spending without judging it. Less is not always better.

## Further out

Understanding comes first. Growing money comes next, using the same foundation: investments, savings goals, and the decisions that move money toward them. The [roadmap](/roadmap) lists what is planned.
