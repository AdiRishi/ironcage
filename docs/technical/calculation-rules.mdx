These rules implement the metrics in [Financial records and metrics](/technical/financial-model). The financial API and processor use the same functions in `packages/finance/src/`. `finance-v1` identifies these rules in stored results. Money examples are fictional.

## Financial event construction

An accepted posting records a signed account-balance change. A financial event groups postings representing one economic event. Its nonnegative magnitude is divided into allocations. Cost queries select cost allocations and subtract their linked credits. Bank balances always use postings.

| Event                   | Posting membership                                                        | Allocation and reporting account                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purchase                | One debit.                                                                | Cost or explicit nonPersonal portions. Reports the charged account.                       |
| Income                  | One established income credit.                                            | Income. Reports the receiving account.                                                    |
| Transfer                | Equal opposite postings between confirmed owned accounts in one currency. | Transfer. Reports the sending account.                                                    |
| Card settlement         | Deposit debit and matching card credit.                                   | Transfer. Reports the deposit account.                                                    |
| Borrowing               | Loan debit and optional matching deposit credit.                          | Borrowing. Reports the loan.                                                              |
| Refund or reimbursement | One established credit.                                                   | Refund or reimbursement, with separate cost_credits links. Reports the receiving account. |
| Financing cost          | One interest or fee debit.                                                | Cost. Reports the charged account.                                                        |
| Loan payment            | Deposit debit and matching loan credit.                                   | Transfer. Reports the loan.                                                               |
| Unresolved              | One posting with an unknown role.                                         | Unresolved. Reports the posting account.                                                  |

Each event's magnitude is the absolute primary posting amount. Paired transfers, settlements, and repayments use the amount moved once. Interest and fee postings remain separate financing-cost events, even when the bank charges them on a repayment date.

Posting-date queries use each event's primary posting date. Spending-date queries use an evidenced purchase date for purchases, otherwise the primary posting date. Credits reduce linked costs on the cost's selected basis. Unlinked credits remain separate unresolved adjustments.

### Loan repayment effects

`principalRepaid` reports the net repayment effect for a loan over the selected posting-date period: confirmed repayments minus interest and fees charged in that period. The display label is **Net principal reduction from repayments**. This is a signed period measure, not an attribution of interest to an individual payment. A negative value is labelled **Financing costs exceed repayments**.

For repayments of `390000` and interest of `280000` in the same period, the result is `110000`. If interest posts on August 31 and repayment on September 2, August retains the `280000` cost and September retains the `390000` repayment. Linking the settlement never moves the interest date. Repayments use the loan-side credit posting date. The deposit-side debit retains its own date for cash calculations. Multiple repayments add within their own dates. New borrowing, interest refunds, and other balance adjustments are shown separately and reconcile the repayment effect to the actual change in loan balance.

A `LoanBreakdown` contains `{accountId, repayments: Money, interest: Money, fees: Money, newBorrowing: Money, otherAdjustments: Money, netRepaymentEffect: Money | null, balanceChange: Money | null}`. OtherAdjustments uses signed balance changes; newBorrowing is a nonnegative magnitude. With complete coverage and balance anchors, `balanceChange = netRepaymentEffect - newBorrowing + otherAdjustments`. Unresolved loan movements or incomplete period coverage make netRepaymentEffect unavailable, with the known components retained. BalanceChange additionally requires both anchors.

Bank-backed pairing requires confirmed ownership, equal opposite values, and corroborating references or user confirmation. Date proximity and amount alone produce a proposal. A missing counterpart leaves its posting visible and the relationship reviewable. Corrections cannot pull a financing-cost posting into a transfer event.

Category, merchant, and reporting-account groups partition cost allocations. Tags and personal events can overlap. Filters deduplicate allocation IDs. Gross purchase count counts distinct purchase event IDs, including fully refunded purchases, and excludes income, repayments, and credit events.

## Comparison execution

`AnalysisDefinition` is `{kind: "financial", query: FinancialQuery}`, `{kind: "forecast", forecast: ForecastDefinition}`, `{kind: "scenario", baseline: ForecastDefinition, overrides: ScenarioOverride[]}`, or `{kind: "computation", question: string, scope: FinancialQuery}`.

The server normalizes a definition and hashes its canonical encoding. A `SnapshotManifest` contains `{schemaVersion: 1, revision, contextGeneration, definitionHash, capturedAt, rowCounts: {table, count}[], evidence: EvidenceRef[], coverage, contentHash}`. ContentHash covers the canonical snapshot payload with that hash field omitted. The selected range includes all linked credits and supporting counterpart postings, even when those records were posted later or outside the displayed period.

Comparisons use inclusive starts and exclusive ends. Calendar weeks begin Monday. Previous-period shifts use the same number of calendar units for week/month/quarter/year selections. A custom range shifts by its day count. Previous-year shifts clamp February 29 to February 28 when necessary. Elapsed alignment caps each compared period at matching elapsed calendar days, with month-end clamping. Full alignment uses the complete requested periods. Neither mode fills unobserved days with zero.

Normalization defaults to `total`. `dailyAverage` is available for grossCosts, netPersonalCosts, income, and surplus. It divides each period's total and each contributor by that period's calendar-day count after elapsed alignment. Daily-average values use `DecimalMoney`, retain exact rational arithmetic until decimal output, and carry unit `perDay`. Any incomplete requested coverage makes that period's daily average unavailable; the result still exposes its known total and coverage. Missing days never shrink the denominator. Frequency decomposition is available only with total normalization.

Absolute delta is current minus previous. Relative change is delta divided by previous only when previous is nonzero. Zero prior values produce null relative change. Categories with no rows but complete coverage contribute zero. Categories with missing coverage carry an incomplete flag.

Contributor rows sort by absolute delta descending, then stable group key. The default display includes 10 rows. Remainder equals total delta minus the displayed partitioned rows. Negative contributors retain their sign. Overlapping tag/event breakdowns do not produce a misleading additive remainder.

Frequency contribution is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2`. Average-cost contribution is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`. Both use exact rational intermediate arithmetic. Rounded contributions use largest-remainder allocation, with a stable component key to break ties. Their sum equals the rounded displayed delta. Average amount is unavailable when its period has zero purchases.

## Ordinary costs

The variable baseline is the median weekly cost over the last 13 fully covered weeks, converted to a monthly equivalent with `365.2425 / 12 / 7`. Confirmed schedule costs are removed from those observations and added back as time-allocated obligations. Explicitly excluded personal-event allocations are omitted from the baseline, with their IDs in evidence.

Weekly and fortnightly commitments use 52.1775 and 26.08875 occurrences per year. Monthly, quarterly, and annual schedules use 12, 4, and 1. A commitment occurrence with an explicit service period allocates its cost by actual days in the intersection with the query period, divided by all days in its service period. A recurring schedule without service-period evidence uses the cadence equivalents above and identifies that assumption. Explicit occurrence allocations replace, rather than add to, the equivalent allocation for the same obligation. Cash timing remains the scheduled date. Loan payments and card settlements are excluded from ordinary costs; separately specified financing costs can be allocated as costs. Fewer than six covered weeks produces a limited baseline with its sample size. Actual cost totals remain available separately.

## Schedule contract and detection

```ts
type ScheduleDefinition = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  role: "cost" | "income" | "cardSettlement" | "loanPayment";
  currency: Currency;
  accountId: Id<"account">;
  counterpartyAccountId: Id<"account"> | null;
  amount:
    | { kind: "fixed"; minor: Minor }
    | { kind: "variable"; expectedMinor: Minor; lowerMinor: Minor; upperMinor: Minor };
  cadence: "weekly" | "fortnightly" | "monthly" | "quarterly" | "annual";
  anchorDate: CalendarDate;
  monthEnd: boolean;
  weekendShift: "none" | "previousBusinessDay" | "nextBusinessDay";
  endDate: CalendarDate | null;
  categoryId: Id<"category"> | null;
};
```

`ScheduleOccurrence` is `{commitmentId, segmentId, nominalDate, scheduledDate, servicePeriod: Range | null, expected: Money, status: "expected" | "matched" | "skipped", eventId: Id<"event"> | null}`. An occurrence stores an optional evidenced or user-confirmed servicePeriod independently of the schedule's effective interval. AccountId is the charged or receiving account. CardSettlement and loanPayment schedules use a deposit account and require the receiving card or loan in counterpartyAccountId. Other roles require a null counterparty. These accounts belong to the effective segment, so changing payment accounts preserves past occurrences. Calendar stepping uses the anchor's day or the last day of the destination month. Weekend adjustment uses weekdays only. Public-holiday changes require an evidenced exception or corrected date.

Detector candidates group by merchant, service, currency, reporting account, and allocation role. They require at least three observations for weekly through quarterly cadence, and two for annual cadence. At least 80% of observed intervals must fit expected dates within three days, or seven days for annual payments. Fixed-amount tolerance is the greater of 100 minor units or 5% of the median. Other consistent series produce variable-amount proposals.

Automatic detection creates proposed commitments. Confirmation or an explicit user schedule makes one active. Forecasts list proposed schedules separately and include them only through an explicit assumption. A missing payment does not end the schedule. Confirmed changes create a new effective segment.

## Forecast contract

`ForecastDefinition` contains the following fields:

| Field                        | Type                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| schemaVersion                | `1`                                                                        |
| asOf                         | `CalendarDate`                                                             |
| horizonDays                  | `30 \| 90 \| 365`                                                          |
| accountIds                   | `Id<"account">[]`                                                          |
| currency                     | `Currency`                                                                 |
| includeProposedCommitmentIds | `Id<"commitment">[]`                                                       |
| variableAccounts             | `{categoryId, shares: {accountId, weight: Decimal}[]}[]`                   |
| oneOffs                      | `{date, accountId, role: "cost" \| "income" \| "credit", amount: Money}[]` |
| cardPayments                 | `CardPaymentAssumption[]`                                                  |
| loans                        | `LoanAssumption[]`                                                         |

`CardPaymentAssumption` contains `{cardAccountId, depositAccountId, nextPaymentDate, cadence, mode: "fullStatement" | "fixed", fixedMinor: Minor | null, statement: {closingDate: CalendarDate, dueDate: CalendarDate, balanceMinor: Minor} | null, cycleCloseDay: number | null, dueDaysAfterClose: number | null}`. Fixed mode requires a nonnegative fixedMinor. FullStatement mode requires a nonnegative debt magnitude for balanceMinor, a cycleCloseDay from 1 to 31, and a nonnegative dueDaysAfterClose. Calendar days clamp at month end. The statement balance covers purchases through closingDate. Subsequent cycles include new posted purchases and subtract credits and payments allocated to that cycle. Settlement never exceeds outstanding debt. Without a full-statement cycle, the user can provide a fixed-payment assumption. A missing starting card balance still makes balance-dependent settlement timing unavailable, even in fixed mode; the known proposed payment amount remains visible. Fixed payments are capped at known outstanding debt.

`LoanAssumption` contains `{loanAccountId, repaymentCommitmentId, monthlyInterestMinor: Minor, monthlyFeesMinor: Minor, chargeDay: number}`. Amounts are nonnegative, chargeDay is 1 through 31 with month-end clamping, and the commitment is an included loanPayment schedule. These are user-confirmed fixed monthly financing-cost assumptions. They charge the loan balance independently of repayments. The first projected charge uses the next charge date after the last covered loan date, including any estimated gap before asOf. An accepted historical charge on that date replaces the estimate. This version does not recalculate interest from rates or offset balances. The result discloses that limitation and the assumed amounts. Missing assumptions leave loan balances and principal projections unavailable; the confirmed cash repayment schedule remains visible.

Starting balances come from the latest reconciled anchor on or before asOf, rolled forward through accepted postings. AsOf is the exclusive end of observed inputs and the inclusive start of the forecast horizon. End-of-day anchors are normalized to the start of the next calendar day. A remaining uncovered gap is simulated once and labelled estimated. Dates already covered by accepted postings are not simulated again. Missing anchors make that account's balance unavailable. Combined cash is unavailable if any included deposit account is unavailable. Schedules and known account components remain visible. AccountIds select reported accounts; snapshot extraction also includes the counterpart accounts needed to simulate their settlements.

### Variable costs and account assignment

Training uses the last 13 fully covered weeks before asOf. At least six weeks are required. Training removes matched scheduled cost events and explicitly excluded personal-event allocations. It uses gross variable purchases because refunds and reimbursements have separate cash timing. Future credits enter only as explicit oneOff credit cash assumptions. A credit changes the account balance and appears separately from earned income and projected gross costs. OneOff amounts are nonnegative; cost charges an eligible deposit or card account, income credits a deposit account, and credit credits an eligible deposit or card account. Scenario costDelta compares projected gross costs including financing costs; assumed credit deltas are reported separately as creditDelta. A cash credit does not retrospectively alter an official historical metric. Historical net personal costs remain unchanged.

Each variable category has explicit payment-account shares. A saved assumption takes precedence. Otherwise the server proposes shares from gross purchases in the training period and records the accepted shares in the normalized definition. Weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Only deposit and card accounts in the same currency are eligible. The user must confirm a proposed allocation before it drives a cash forecast. A user startAnalysis command can supply that allocation explicitly. User-saved analysis or scenario definitions retain accepted shares. Agent requests may reuse those shares; new or changed shares require a clarification and an accepted definition through saveAnalysis or saveScenario. Missing shares leave cash timing unavailable for affected categories. Changes in payment habits can replace shares without changing the category's combined spending baseline.

A predicted purchase charges its allocated deposit account immediately or increases its allocated card debt. A scheduled settlement debits its deposit account and credits its card or loan once. Schedule occurrences and cardPayments cannot both settle the same card cycle. Loan repayments are sourced only from their commitment. LoanAssumption interest and fees replace any cost schedules for those same loan charges; overlapping schedule definitions return InvalidInput. Loan repayments cannot exceed projected outstanding debt, and a settled loan stops projected financing charges. Nonnegative cost amounts split by account shares with largest-remainder rounding and a stable account-ID tie break.

### Prediction paths

The initial model samples complete observed training weeks, preserving daily category amounts and their joint variation. It performs no mean-centering, median adjustment, or zero clipping. Seven zero-spend weeks and six `10000` weeks therefore produce sampled weeks of zero or `10000`, never an intermediate `5385`.

A candidate exponential-smoothing model uses alpha from `{0.2, 0.5, 0.8}`, chosen with pre-cutoff rolling evaluation. Each category's weekly level starts at its first training observation and updates `level = alpha * observed + (1 - alpha) * level`. Candidate paths multiply sampled daily category values by final level divided by the category's training-week mean. A zero training mean produces zero paths. This preserves nonnegative amounts. Selection and calibration follow the gates below.

Each result uses 1,000 paths. For each future calendar week, a path samples one training week uniformly with replacement; a partial week uses its matching weekdays. The same sampled week is used across all categories. A deterministic seed derives from the input snapshot and normalized baseline definition. Account assignment, explicit scenario scaling, one-offs, schedules, and settlements then transform each path in that order. VariableCostScale multiplies the entire sampled category path, so factor zero removes that category's variable purchases. Known commitments enter once after variable training exclusions.

The reported point forecast is the empirical P50 of simulated balances. P10 and P90 come from those same joint paths. They are not sums of category quantiles. Exact arithmetic and largest-remainder allocation preserve money through account splits. Quantiles use the nearest-rank convention with rank `ceil(p * pathCount)` in ascending order. [Forecast distributions](https://otexts.com/fpp3/prediction-intervals.html)

## Scenarios and evaluation

`ScenarioOverride` is `{kind: "variableCostScale", categoryId, factor: Decimal}`, `{kind: "commitmentAmount", commitmentId, minor: Minor, effectiveFrom: CalendarDate}`, or `{kind: "oneOff", date, accountId, role: "cost" | "income" | "credit", amount: Money}`. Factors must be nonnegative and at most 10. Baseline and scenario run on the same snapshot and random seed. Related substitutions are separate explicit overrides.

`ForecastEvaluation` contains `{target: "terminalCashBalance", cutoffCount, nonOverlappingCutoffCount, horizonDays, meanAbsoluteError: Money, bias: DecimalMoney, intervalCoverage: Decimal | null, meanIntervalWidth: Money | null, meanIntervalScore: Money | null, baselineMeanAbsoluteError: Money, baselineMeanIntervalScore: Money | null, method: "asKnown" | "retrospective"}`. Errors compare the forecast's terminal cash P50 with observed combined cash for the same included deposit accounts. Cutoffs lacking reconciled target balances are excluded and counted in evaluation notes. Bias is forecast minus observation.

Rolling cutoffs train only on earlier records and assumptions available at that cutoff. Retrospective tests may use historical transactions uploaded later, but cannot use future transactions to detect schedules or tune parameters. As-known tests also respect import, correction, and assumption-acceptance timestamps. Overlapping cutoffs contribute descriptive errors; the calibration subset uses chronological non-overlapping target windows, selecting the earliest eligible cutoff and then the next cutoff at least horizonDays later. Coverage is assessed separately at 30, 90, and 365 days. Calendar history length alone does not establish comparable evaluation coverage.

Evaluation money outputs use half-even rounding to minor units; bias retains DecimalMoney precision. All gates compare unrounded errors.

The candidate replaces direct week resampling only with at least six eligible cutoffs, lower mean absolute error, and no larger absolute bias. The default P10/P90 interval has nominal coverage 0.8. It is labelled calibrated only with at least 20 non-overlapping cutoffs, empirical coverage between 0.75 and 0.85 inclusive, and a 95% Wilson interval containing 0.8. The candidate's mean interval score must also be no worse than the direct-resampling baseline on the same cutoffs. Failure of any gate leaves the interval uncalibrated. Unsupported inputs make it unavailable.

The interval score is width plus ten times the distance of an observation below P10 or above P90, otherwise width alone. The result retains coverage, width, score, sample counts, and evaluation mode. Calibration describes the tested account scope, horizon, and regime; it does not transfer automatically to changed assumptions or another currency. Historical model selection and calibration use separate held-out cutoffs. These are provisional promotion thresholds, subject to the decision policy in [Technical](/technical#design-status). [Interval scoring](https://otexts.com/fpp3/distaccuracy.html), [rolling-origin evaluation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)

## Insight detectors and ranking

`detectors-v1` defines these initial rules for fully covered comparable periods:

| Detector               | Candidate condition                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recurring price change | Two successive payments exceed the prior three-payment median by both 10% and 100 minor units.                                        |
| Category cost drift    | Two consecutive complete months exceed the preceding six-month median by both 20% and 5000 AUD minor units.                           |
| Repeated fees          | At least three fee events in 90 days and at least 2000 AUD minor units total.                                                         |
| Unusual purchase       | Purchase exceeds three times the merchant/category median and at least 10000 AUD minor units, with at least ten comparison purchases. |
| Commitment lapse       | A confirmed expected occurrence is more than seven days overdue, with complete source coverage through that date.                     |

Thresholds in other currencies require configured equivalents. A detector with insufficient history returns no candidate and records the reason. An LLM cannot lower a threshold to manufacture an insight.

The fingerprint hashes detector version, subject IDs, metric, and change-start date. Reprocessing updates that finding's result instead of creating another. A dismissed finding reopens only for a new change interval or at least 50% and 5000 AUD minor units of additional impact. Superseded evidence closes the old finding.

Rank is an integer from 0 to 100: material impact up to 50 points, persistence up to 20, novelty up to 20, and explicit user-interest match up to 10. Impact contributes one point per 1000 AUD minor units, capped at 50. Each additional corroborating period adds five persistence points. Previously unseen subjects receive 20 novelty points, previously useful subjects 10, and dismissed unchanged subjects zero and remain suppressed. These scores order findings, not probabilities.

`IntentionDefinition` contains `{baseline: FinancialQuery, current: FinancialQuery, startDate: CalendarDate, relatedCosts: FinancialQuery | null, note: string | null}`. Follow-through reports the same metric across those periods, includes related costs only when selected, and describes change without claiming causality.
