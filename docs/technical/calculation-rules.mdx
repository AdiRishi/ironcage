These rules implement the metrics in [Financial records and metrics](/technical/financial-model). The financial API and processor use the same functions in `packages/finance/src/`. `finance-v1` identifies these rules in stored results. Money examples are fictional.

## Financial event construction

An accepted posting records a signed account-balance change. A financial event groups postings representing one economic event. Its nonnegative magnitude is divided into allocations. Cost queries select cost allocations and subtract their linked credits. Bank balances always use postings.

| Event                   | Posting membership and magnitude                                                                                                            | Allocations and reporting account                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purchase                | One debit. Magnitude is its absolute amount.                                                                                                | Cost, or explicit nonPersonal portions. Reports the charged account.                                                                     |
| Income                  | One credit established as income.                                                                                                           | Income. Reports the receiving account.                                                                                                   |
| Transfer                | Equal-and-opposite postings between confirmed owned accounts, same currency.                                                                | One transfer allocation. Reports the sending account.                                                                                    |
| Card settlement         | Deposit debit and card-liability credit of the same amount.                                                                                 | Transfer allocation. Reports the paying deposit account.                                                                                 |
| Borrowing               | Loan-liability debit and optional matching deposit credit.                                                                                  | Borrowing allocation. Reports the loan.                                                                                                  |
| Refund or reimbursement | Credit posting.                                                                                                                             | Refund or reimbursement allocation with cost_credits links. Reports the receiving account.                                               |
| Financing cost          | Unpaired bank interest or fee debit.                                                                                                        | Cost allocation with interest or fee category. Reports the charged account.                                                              |
| Loan payment            | Cash debit, matching loan credit, and only interest/fee postings evidenced as part of that repayment period. Magnitude is the cash payment. | Cost portions plus principal remainder. Reports the loan for principal, and the loan as the event's reporting account for cost grouping. |
| Unresolved              | Established posting with unknown financial role.                                                                                            | Unresolved allocation. Reports the posting account.                                                                                      |

For a cash repayment of `390000`, a loan credit of `390000`, and interest debit of `280000`, the loan balance improves by `110000`. One loanPayment event has cost `280000` and principal `110000`. Its interest posting cannot belong to another event. If statement evidence does not establish the interest/payment relationship, the app reports the known cash outflow and financing costs, while principal decomposition remains unresolved.

Bank-backed transfer pairing requires known owned accounts, equal opposite values, and corroborating references or user confirmation. Date proximity and matching amount alone create a proposal. Loan fees exceeding a payment, new drawdowns, and multiple repayments require an explicitly validated grouping. Negative principal is not silently clamped to zero.

Posting-date queries use the primary posting date for event allocations. Spending-date queries use an evidenced purchase date, otherwise that posting date. Credits reduce linked costs on the cost's selected date basis. Unlinked credits remain separate unresolved adjustments.

Category, merchant, and reporting-account groups partition cost allocations. Tag and personal-event groups can overlap and are labelled accordingly. A query selecting multiple tags counts a matching allocation once. Gross purchase count counts distinct purchase event IDs, including fully refunded purchases, and excludes income, repayments, and credit events.

## Comparison execution

`AnalysisDefinition` is `{kind: "financial", query: FinancialQuery}`, `{kind: "forecast", forecast: ForecastDefinition}`, `{kind: "scenario", baseline: ForecastDefinition, overrides: ScenarioOverride[]}`, or `{kind: "computation", question: string, scope: FinancialQuery}`.

The server normalizes a definition and hashes its canonical encoding. A `SnapshotManifest` contains `{schemaVersion: 1, revision, definitionHash, capturedAt, rowCounts: {table, count}[], evidence: EvidenceRef[], coverage, contentHash}`. ContentHash covers the canonical snapshot payload with that hash field omitted. The selected range includes all linked credits and supporting counterpart postings, even when those records were posted later or outside the displayed period.

Comparisons use inclusive starts and exclusive ends. Calendar weeks begin Monday. Previous-period shifts use the same number of calendar units for week/month/quarter/year selections. A custom range shifts by its day count. Previous-year shifts clamp February 29 to February 28 when necessary. Elapsed alignment caps each compared period at matching elapsed calendar days, with month-end clamping. Full alignment uses the complete requested periods. Neither mode fills unobserved days with zero.

Absolute delta is current minus previous. Relative change is delta divided by previous only when previous is nonzero. Zero prior values produce null relative change. Categories with no rows but complete coverage contribute zero. Categories with missing coverage carry an incomplete flag.

Contributor rows sort by absolute delta descending, then stable group key. The default display includes 10 rows. Remainder equals total delta minus the displayed partitioned rows. Negative contributors retain their sign. Overlapping tag/event breakdowns do not produce a misleading additive remainder.

Frequency contribution is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2`. Average-cost contribution is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`. Both use exact rational intermediate arithmetic. Rounded contributions use largest-remainder allocation, with a stable component key to break ties. Their sum equals the rounded displayed delta. Average amount is unavailable when its period has zero purchases.

## Ordinary costs

The variable baseline is the median weekly cost over the last 13 fully covered weeks, converted to a monthly equivalent with `365.2425 / 12 / 7`. Confirmed schedule costs are removed from those observations and added back as time-allocated obligations. Explicitly excluded personal-event allocations are omitted from the baseline, with their IDs in evidence.

Weekly and fortnightly commitments use 52.1775 and 26.08875 occurrences per year. Monthly, quarterly, and annual schedules use 12, 4, and 1. A commitment with an explicit service period allocates by actual covered days instead. Fewer than six covered weeks produces a limited baseline with its sample size. Actual cost totals remain available separately.

## Schedule contract and detection

```ts
type ScheduleDefinition = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  role: "cost" | "income" | "cardSettlement" | "loanPayment";
  currency: Currency;
  amount:
    | { kind: "fixed"; minor: Minor }
    | { kind: "variable"; expectedMinor: Minor; lowerMinor: Minor; upperMinor: Minor };
  cadence: "weekly" | "fortnightly" | "monthly" | "quarterly" | "annual";
  anchorDate: Date;
  monthEnd: boolean;
  weekendShift: "none" | "previousBusinessDay" | "nextBusinessDay";
  endDate: Date | null;
  categoryId: Id<"category"> | null;
};
```

`ScheduleOccurrence` is `{commitmentId, scheduledDate, expected: Money, status: "expected" | "matched" | "skipped", eventId: Id<"event"> | null}`. Calendar stepping uses the anchor's day or the last day of the destination month. Weekend adjustment uses weekdays only. Public-holiday changes require an evidenced exception or corrected date.

Detector candidates group by merchant, service, currency, reporting account, and allocation role. They require at least three observations for weekly through quarterly cadence, and two for annual cadence. At least 80% of observed intervals must fit expected dates within three days, or seven days for annual payments. Fixed-amount tolerance is the greater of 100 minor units or 5% of the median. Other consistent series produce variable-amount proposals.

Automatic detection creates proposed commitments. Confirmation or an explicit user schedule makes one active. Forecasts list proposed schedules separately and include them only through an explicit assumption. A missing payment does not end the schedule. Confirmed changes create a new effective segment.

## Forecast contract

`ForecastDefinition` contains `{schemaVersion: 1, asOf: Date, horizonDays: 30 | 90 | 365, accountIds, currency, includeProposedCommitmentIds, oneOffs: {date, accountId, role: "cost" | "income", amount: Money}[], cardPayments: {cardAccountId, depositAccountId, nextPaymentDate, cadence, mode: "fullStatement" | "fixed", fixedMinor: Minor | null, statement: {closingDate: Date, dueDate: Date, balanceMinor: Minor} | null, cycleCloseDay: number | null}[]}`.

Starting balances come from the latest reconciled anchor on or before asOf, rolled forward through accepted postings. A remaining gap is simulated and labelled estimated. Missing anchors make cash balances unavailable for that account. They do not create a zero starting balance.

The baseline uses the last 13 covered weeks after excluding scheduled events and explicit one-offs. At least six weeks are required for a data-derived variable-spend forecast. Otherwise the result exposes known schedules and missing assumptions, with no calibrated prediction interval.

For each category, the baseline weekly total is the median training-week total. Its weekday shares are each weekday's aggregate cost divided by total training cost. A zero-cost category predicts zero. The daily point forecast is weekly total times weekday share, rounded with largest-remainder allocation over each week.

The initial variable model is this weekly weekday-profile baseline. A candidate exponential-smoothing model uses alpha selected from `{0.2, 0.5, 0.8}` by historical absolute error. For each alpha, exponential smoothing starts at the first training-week total and updates `level = alpha * observed + (1 - alpha) * level` in chronological order. The final level replaces the median weekly total. Selection uses only pre-cutoff data. The better evaluated candidate becomes the configured model version.

Prediction paths resample whole historical weeks across categories together. Each sampled residual is observed daily category cost minus that weekday/category training mean. The residual is added to the point forecast, with costs floored at zero. Sampling the same week across categories preserves observed co-movement. Known schedules are added after this variable-cost calculation. Each forecast uses 1,000 paths and a deterministic seed derived from snapshot hash and definition hash. Known commitments are added once. The output reports empirical P10, P50, and P90 cash balances by date, not a sum of independent category quantiles.

Estimated card purchases increase card liability. A card-payment schedule moves cash from its deposit account and reduces the liability. Cost is recognized at purchase, not again at settlement. Full-statement settlement requires a statement balance and cycle definition. Without them, the forecast asks for a fixed-payment assumption or marks cash timing unresolved. Loan-payment assumptions use confirmed repayment schedules and evidenced cost/principal rules.

## Scenarios and evaluation

`ScenarioOverride` is `{kind: "variableCostScale", categoryId, factor: Decimal}`, `{kind: "commitmentAmount", commitmentId, minor: Minor, effectiveFrom: Date}`, or `{kind: "oneOff", date, accountId, role: "cost" | "income", amount: Money}`. Factors must be nonnegative and at most 10. Baseline and scenario run on the same snapshot and random seed. Related substitutions are separate explicit overrides.

`ForecastEvaluation` contains `{cutoffCount, horizonDays, meanAbsoluteError: Money, bias: Money, intervalCoverage: Decimal | null, baselineMeanAbsoluteError: Money, method: "asKnown" | "retrospective"}`. Rolling cutoffs train only on prior data. Historical uploads that arrived together permit retrospective transaction-date backtests, but cannot prove what the app knew before upload. As-known tests also respect import and correction availability timestamps.

A candidate replaces the simple baseline only with at least six cutoffs, lower mean absolute error, and no larger absolute bias. Prediction intervals are labelled uncalibrated until at least 20 held-out points exist for that horizon. Evaluation is stored alongside the model version. This gate chooses among complete implemented methods, not a reason to omit forecasting.

## Insight detectors and ranking

`detectors-v1` defines these initial rules for fully covered comparable periods:

| Detector               | Candidate condition                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recurring price change | Two successive payments exceed the prior three-payment median by both 10% and 100 minor units.                                        |
| Category cost drift    | Two consecutive complete months exceed the preceding six-month median by both 20% and 5000 AUD minor units.                           |
| Repeated fees          | At least three fee events in 90 days and at least 2000 AUD minor units total.                                                         |
| Unusual purchase       | Purchase exceeds three times the merchant/category median and at least 10000 AUD minor units, with at least ten comparison purchases. |
| Commitment lapse       | A confirmed expected occurrence is more than seven days overdue, with complete source coverage through that date.                     |

Thresholds in other currencies require configured equivalents. A detector with insufficient history returns no candidate and records the reason. An LLM cannot lower a threshold to manufacture an insight.

The fingerprint hashes detector version, subject IDs, metric, and change-start date. Reprocessing updates that finding's result instead of creating another. A dismissed finding reopens only for a new change interval or at least 50% and 5000 AUD minor units of additional impact. Superseded evidence closes the old finding.

Rank is an integer from 0 to 100: material impact up to 50 points, persistence up to 20, novelty up to 20, and explicit user-interest match up to 10. Impact contributes one point per 1000 AUD minor units, capped at 50. Each additional corroborating period adds five persistence points. Previously unseen subjects receive 20 novelty points, previously useful subjects 10, and dismissed unchanged subjects zero and remain suppressed. These scores order findings, not probabilities.

`IntentionDefinition` contains `{baseline: FinancialQuery, current: FinancialQuery, startDate: Date, relatedCosts: FinancialQuery | null, note: string | null}`. Follow-through reports the same metric across those periods, includes related costs only when selected, and describes change without claiming causality.
