The API and the processor use the same functions in `packages/finance/src/`. Every result records the calculation version that produced it. Money examples are fictional and in minor units unless a dollar sign appears.

## Financial event construction

Stage 3. An accepted posting records a signed balance change. A financial event groups the postings of one economic event, has a nonnegative magnitude, and divides that magnitude into allocations. Cost queries select cost allocations and subtract their credit links. Balance queries use postings only.

| Event kind                | Posting membership                                            | Allocation role and reporting account                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `purchase`                | One debit.                                                    | `purchase`, optionally with a `nonPersonal` portion. Reports the charged account.                  |
| `income`                  | One credit.                                                   | `income`. Reports the receiving account.                                                           |
| `transfer`                | One or two movements between owned endpoints in one currency. | `transfer`. Reports the sending account when present, otherwise the receiving one.                 |
| `cardSettlement`          | Deposit debit, card credit, or the matched pair.              | `transfer`. Reports the deposit account when present, otherwise the card.                          |
| `borrowing`               | Loan debit, deposit credit, or the matched pair.              | `borrowing`. Reports the loan.                                                                     |
| `loanPayment`             | Deposit debit, loan credit, or the matched pair.              | `transfer`. Reports the loan.                                                                      |
| `refund`, `reimbursement` | One credit.                                                   | `refund` or `reimbursement`, applied to costs through credit links. Reports the receiving account. |
| `financingCost`           | One interest or fee debit.                                    | `financingCost`. Reports the charged account.                                                      |
| `unresolved`              | One posting whose role is unknown.                            | `unresolved`. Reports the posting's account.                                                       |

Magnitude is the absolute amount of the primary posting. A matched pair uses the amount moved once. Interest and fees stay separate financing-cost events even when charged on a repayment date.

`MovementLink` is `{ from: Endpoint, to: Endpoint, evidence: ObservationId[] }` where `Endpoint` is `{ kind: "account", accountId }` or `{ kind: "external", label }`. An external endpoint is one the user confirmed they own; it has no balance. Pairing two postings needs equal opposite amounts in one currency plus corroboration: a matching reference in both descriptions, or user confirmation. Amount and date proximity alone produce a proposal, never a link.

A fee association records that a `financingCost` event relates to a purchase, as proposed or confirmed. Both events keep their own amounts and dates and both count.

### Date basis

`posted` uses each event's primary posting date. `spending` uses the purchase date for purchases when established, otherwise the posting date. A credit link reduces the cost in the cost's period on the selected basis; the credit's own posting keeps its date in account views. Unlinked credits appear as separate unresolved adjustments.

### Loan repayment effects

`netPrincipalReduction` for a loan over a posted-date period is confirmed loan-side repayments minus interest and fees charged in that period. Repayments of 390000 and interest of 280000 give 110000. If interest posts on 31 August and the repayment on 2 September, August has the 280000 cost and September the 390000 repayment. A negative value is labelled "Financing costs exceed repayments".

```ts
type LoanBreakdown = {
  accountId: Id<"account">;
  repayments: Money;
  interest: Money;
  fees: Money;
  newBorrowing: Money;
  otherAdjustments: Money;
  netRepaymentEffect: Money | null;
  balanceChange: Money | null;
};
```

With full coverage and both anchors, `balanceChange = netRepaymentEffect - newBorrowing + otherAdjustments`. Unresolved loan movements or a coverage gap make `netRepaymentEffect` null with the known components still filled. `balanceChange` also needs both anchors.

### Grouping

Category, merchant, and reporting account partition cost allocations. Tags and personal events overlap; a query selecting several tags counts each allocation once. Purchase count counts distinct purchase event IDs, including fully refunded ones.

## Comparisons

Stage 4. A query is `{ measure, period, comparison, basis, currency, accounts, filters, normalization }`. Filters within one dimension combine with OR, dimensions with AND, and a category selection includes its descendants.

### Periods

Periods are `[start, endExclusive)`. Relative selections support rolling day counts and calendar weeks, months, quarters, and years. Weeks start Monday. A relative selection resolves once per request in the settings timezone. The current calendar period defaults to elapsed alignment: both periods end at the same elapsed day count, with month-end clamping. Full alignment uses whole periods. Fixed dates never move.

The comparison shift uses the selection's unit. A day-count selection shifts by its day count. A calendar selection must span a whole number of units and shifts by that many. A previous-year shift clamps 29 February to 28 February. Neither alignment fills unobserved days with zero; missing coverage is reported.

### Deltas and contributors

Absolute delta is current minus previous. Relative change is delta over previous only when previous is nonzero; a zero prior gives a null relative change and the UI shows the new amount. A group with complete coverage and no rows contributes zero. A group with missing coverage carries an incomplete flag.

Contributor rows sort by absolute delta descending, then by group key. The default shows ten rows plus a remainder equal to the total delta minus the shown rows. Overlapping groups such as tags show no remainder because their rows are not additive.

Frequency contribution is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2` and average-cost contribution is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`, where `n` is purchase count and `p` is average purchase amount. Intermediate values are exact rationals. Rounded contributions use largest-remainder allocation with a stable key so they sum to the rounded delta. Average amount is unavailable when a period has zero purchases. Frequency decomposition needs total normalization.

### Daily average

`dailyAverage` applies to gross costs, net personal costs, income, and surplus. It divides each period's total and each contributor by the period's calendar days after alignment, as `DecimalMoney` per day. Any missing coverage inside the period makes its average unavailable while the total and denominator stay visible. Missing days never shrink the denominator.

### Saved analyses

A saved analysis stores a name and a query. Opening it or pressing refresh runs the query against current records. Relative dates resolve at that time; fixed dates stay fixed. Results are not stored.

## Ordinary costs

Stage 5. The variable baseline is the mean weekly net personal cost over the last 13 fully covered and fully interpreted Monday-to-Monday weeks ending on or before the period's `endExclusive`. Each period or series bucket has its own cutoff. All groups in one estimate share the same week set and account coverage; filters select allocations after the week set is fixed, so group means add to the headline. The monthly conversion factor is `365.2425 / 12 / 7`.

Events matched to confirmed schedule occurrences leave the variable observations and enter once as commitments. Personal events flagged `excludeFromOrdinary` and explicit exclusions remove their allocations. Actual-spending views keep those records.

```ts
type OrdinaryCostBasis = {
  cutoff: CalendarDate;
  weeks: Period[];
  excludedPersonalEventIds: Id<"personalEvent">[];
  matchedEventIds: Id<"event">[];
  sampleStatus: "sufficient" | "limited" | "unavailable";
};
```

Six or more weeks is sufficient. One to five is limited. Zero makes the variable baseline unavailable; the estimate then shows commitments and an unavailable headline.

Commitments convert to a monthly amount by cadence: weekly `52.1775 / 12`, fortnightly `26.08875 / 12`, monthly 1, quarterly `1 / 3`, annual `1 / 12`. A matched occurrence uses its accepted net cost; an unmatched one uses the scheduled amount. An occurrence with a service period allocates its cost by days of the period inside the query period divided by days of the service period, then converts by `365.2425 / 12` over the query period's days. Without a service period, the cadence amount is weighted by the fraction of query days inside the effective segment. Loan payments and card settlements are excluded; financing costs enter as costs.

Each variable allocation contributes its net cost divided by the week count times the monthly factor. Keep rationals until the output boundary, round the headline half-even, then allocate minor units to components by largest remainder with stable IDs. Group totals sum those rounded components.

Worked example: 13 complete weeks with no commitments. Five weeks have a $100 cost in category A, five have $100 in B, and three have both, so the weeks total $1,600. The weekly mean is `1600 / 13 = $123.0769`. Monthly is `123.0769 * 365.2425 / 12 / 7 = $535.15`. Category A contributes `800 / 13 * 4.348125 = $267.5769` and B the same. Rounding to cents gives $267.57 each, one cent short, so largest remainder assigns the cent to the first stable ID: A $267.58, B $267.57, total $535.15.

## Schedules

Stage 5.

```ts
type ScheduleSegment = {
  role: "cost" | "income" | "cardSettlement" | "loanPayment";
  currency: Currency;
  accountId: Id<"account">;
  counterpartyAccountId: Id<"account"> | null;
  amount:
    | { kind: "fixed"; minor: bigint }
    | { kind: "variable"; expectedMinor: bigint; lowerMinor: bigint; upperMinor: bigint };
  cadence: "weekly" | "fortnightly" | "monthly" | "quarterly" | "annual";
  anchorDate: CalendarDate;
  monthEnd: boolean;
  weekendShift: "none" | "previousBusinessDay" | "nextBusinessDay";
  effective: Period;
  categoryId: Id<"category"> | null;
  merchantId: Id<"merchant"> | null;
};

type Occurrence = {
  commitmentId: Id<"commitment">;
  segmentId: Id<"segment">;
  nominalDate: CalendarDate;
  scheduledDate: CalendarDate;
  servicePeriod: Period | null;
  expected: Money;
  status: "expected" | "matched" | "skipped";
  eventId: Id<"event"> | null;
  excludedEventIds: Id<"event">[];
};
```

`cardSettlement` and `loanPayment` segments charge a deposit account and name the card or loan as counterparty. Other roles have a null counterparty. Changing amount or account ends the current segment and starts a new one, so past occurrences keep their segment. Calendar stepping uses the anchor's day of month or the last day when `monthEnd`. Weekend shifts move to the nearest weekday in the chosen direction. Public holidays need an explicit corrected date on the occurrence. Variable amounts require `0 <= lower <= expected <= upper`; estimates use `expected`.

Suggestions come from repeated payments with the same merchant, account, and currency at roughly regular intervals. Show the supporting dates and amounts; the user confirms cadence, amount, and effective start. Tune tolerances against the corpus. A missing payment does not end a schedule.

### Occurrence matching

An automatic match needs a confirmed segment, an event on the segment's account with the role the segment expects, a posted date within three days of `scheduledDate` or seven for annual, and an eligible amount: within the greater of 100 minor units or 5% for fixed amounts, or within `[lower, upper]` for variable. Cost segments compare gross cost allocations, income segments income allocations, settlement and loan segments the movement magnitude. Both sides must have exactly one eligible candidate; otherwise create an interpretation review. One event matches at most one occurrence.

User matches go through the same version-checked command and can override date or amount tolerance, not account, currency, or role. Unmatching adds the event to `excludedEventIds` so automatic matching does not restore it; an explicit match removes it. Event corrections and segment changes recheck affected matches; an invalid match becomes a review item. Match changes bump the commitment version and visible queries refetch.

## Forecasts

Stage 5. A forecast is a 30 or 90 day outlook from a start date. Inputs: currency, selected accounts and categories, confirmed schedules, the recent baseline weeks, explicit personal-event exclusions, and one-off assumptions for costs, income, and credits. The result records the inputs, the calculation time, and coverage limits.

1. Variable costs: mean of up to 13 fully covered and interpreted weeks before the start date, with scheduled payments and excluded events removed. Show the week count and dates. Multiply by `horizonDays / 7`.
2. Add each scheduled cost occurrence inside the horizon once, and each one-off cost once.
3. Expected income is scheduled income plus explicit income assumptions.
4. Credits enter only as explicit assumptions and are shown separately.
5. Projected surplus is income minus costs plus credits, labelled as an estimate.

Keep exact values until output and round there. No covered baseline makes the cost estimate unavailable while the commitment calendar still works.

The cash calendar lists dated movements on deposit accounts from confirmed schedules, including settlements and loan payments. It does not project balances, interest, or card cycles. The latest evidenced balance per account is shown with its date as context.

## Scenarios

Stage 5. A scenario copies the baseline assumptions and changes category amounts, a segment's amount from a date, or dated one-offs. A substitution such as more groceries after less delivery is another explicit change. Both calculations run on the same inputs in one request and the difference is reported as an estimated change in cost, income, or credits. Saving stores the assumptions; opening recalculates. Nothing in a scenario touches postings, events, or confirmed schedules.

## Detectors

Stage 7. Start with three deterministic detectors: repeated fees, a material increase in a recurring payment, and a sustained change in a category's cost across complete comparable periods. Each reports the absolute amount and the percentage where the prior is nonzero. Thresholds live beside the detector code and are tuned on the corpus. Rank by amount, then persistence.

A finding's identity is `(detector, subject, changePeriod)`. A repeat scan updates the same finding and keeps its useful or dismissed state. A new change period creates a new finding. A correction that removes the supporting pattern closes the finding. A detector without enough history produces nothing and, when the user asked for that check, explains what is missing.

An intention stores a measure, a fixed baseline period, a start date, a current-period choice, and optional related measures. Each review computes both periods from current data and reports the dates included. Missing post-start history is insufficient evidence. The result describes observed change without claiming cause.
