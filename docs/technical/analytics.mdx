Analytics takes a financial query and a stable dataset revision. It returns numerical results and evidence before an LLM writes an explanation. The [metric reference](/technical/financial-model#metric-definitions) defines which money belongs in each calculation.

[Calculation rules](/technical/calculation-rules) defines the concrete algorithms, schedule and scenario schemas, forecast sampling, and detector defaults. [Shared contracts](/technical/contracts) defines result payloads.

## Comparisons

Postgres performs indexed filtering and exact aggregation through the financial API. Shared finance functions calculate comparisons, allocation adjustments, and statistical summaries. Live charts, agent tools, and scheduled jobs use these same functions.

Period definitions include date basis, timezone, account scope, filters, and comparison strategy. Partial-month comparisons align elapsed calendar dates. Daily averages use the query's explicit normalization and the denominator and incomplete-coverage rules in Calculation rules. Missing periods do not contribute zero-valued observations.

Category, merchant, and account totals group disjoint cost allocations. Tag and personal-event breakdowns identify overlapping groups. Multi-tag filters deduplicate allocation IDs. Merchant aliases resolve before grouping, while service distinctions such as rides and delivery remain intact.

Absolute differences are computed before percentages. A zero baseline produces an unavailable percentage and an absolute new amount. Decreases remain negative contributions in an explanation of a net increase.

## Frequency and average payment

For one consistent purchase population, total spending equals purchase count times average purchase cost. Refunds adjust the linked purchase cost. They do not add another purchase to the count.

Let count and average cost in the two periods be n0, p0 and n1, p1. The symmetric decomposition divides their interaction evenly:

$$
\Delta S = (n_1-n_0)\frac{p_0+p_1}{2} + (p_1-p_0)\frac{n_0+n_1}{2}
$$

The first term is the frequency contribution. The second is the average-cost contribution. They sum to the exact change before display rounding.

Displayed contributions use a deterministic remainder allocation so their rounded values also sum to the displayed total change. The evidence retains the unrounded calculation.

For 10 purchases averaging $20 and 12 averaging $25, spending rises from $200 to $300. Frequency contributes $45 and average cost contributes $55. This does not establish that unit prices rose.

When either period has no purchases, its average is undefined. The result describes new or ceased activity instead of applying the formula with a fabricated average.

## Ordinary costs and events

The ordinary-cost calculation combines the arithmetic mean of covered weekly variable costs with confirmed recurring obligations. Each estimate uses one week set and rounded contribution population across all groups. Quarterly and annual amounts are allocated over their covered periods. The adjustment never changes source cash dates.

Event exclusions use explicit membership, such as a trip or a one-off home purchase. The result records the included and excluded allocations. Repeated exceptional spending remains visible in actual totals and can generate a separate finding.

Known changes, including mortgage commencement, define meaningful comparison segments. Credit-card migration does not define a spending regime change by itself. Changes in account coverage take precedence over statistical explanations.

## Recurring-cost detection

Candidate series and eligible cadences follow the [schedule detection rules](/technical/calculation-rules#schedule-contract-and-detection). Month-end and weekend posting shifts do not require fixed day counts.

The detector evaluates interval consistency, amount variation, observation count, and missing expected occurrences. Fixed subscriptions and variable bills use different amount tolerances. An inferred schedule retains its evidence and can be replaced by a confirmed schedule.

Effective dates preserve historical prices. A changed subscription amount creates a new schedule segment. A missed occurrence is a candidate issue, not automatic cancellation.

## Change detection and insight ranking

The detectors in [Calculation rules](/technical/calculation-rules#insight-detectors-and-ranking) compare recent periods with historical baselines. Their sample requirements, thresholds, and ranking rules are versioned configuration. A replacement method requires evaluation evidence and an updated specification.

Each candidate records its query, observed change, baseline, sample size, and evidence. Coverage failures suppress unsupported conclusions. Persistence requirements and meaningful absolute amounts reduce chance findings from scanning many categories.

Related candidates share a finding identity based on metric, subject, and change interval. Ranking considers amount affected, persistence, novelty, and user feedback. Reprocessing the same revision does not create another inbox item.

The analyst can investigate a candidate and write its explanation. It cannot turn a statistical association into an established cause. Dismissals and supplied context affect ranking without altering raw history.

## Forecast construction

A forecast combines schedules for known income and commitments with estimates of variable costs. It keeps costs and cash settlement separate, especially for credit cards and loan repayments.

The starting position uses reconciled account anchors. Missing time between the last anchor and today is simulated and labelled estimated. Card purchase estimates increase card liability. The payment schedule determines when deposit cash is reduced. The same purchase is not subtracted twice from cash.

Variable-cost candidates begin with joint resampling of recent fully covered weeks and a multiplicatively adjusted exponential-smoothing candidate. More complex methods are adopted only when they improve held-out results at relevant horizons. [Exponential smoothing](https://otexts.com/fpp3/ses.html) provides a transparent recent-weighted baseline.

Prediction ranges use the joint historical resampling defined in [Calculation rules](/technical/calculation-rules#forecast-contract). Their calibration status is reported separately from the simulated range. Joint samples preserve weekday patterns and category relationships within each sampled week. They do not preserve dependence between successive weeks or seasonal changes. Adding independent category percentiles is not a valid combined prediction interval.

Known commitments are included once. Variable-cost training excludes the same scheduled obligations to prevent double counting. One-off future expenses are explicit scenario inputs.

## Scenarios and evaluation

A scenario contains named overrides to a baseline definition. It never changes transactions or confirmed commitments. A saved comparison retains both definitions and the input revision.

Forecast evaluation uses rolling historical cutoffs. Each run trains on prior records and predicts later periods. As-known evaluation respects import and correction timestamps. Retrospective evaluation uses transaction dates and is labelled separately, as defined in [Calculation rules](/technical/calculation-rules#scenarios-and-evaluation). [Rolling-origin evaluation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)

Evaluation measures absolute error, bias, and interval coverage at the product's forecast horizons. It compares models against simple baselines and separates periods around known structural changes. Usable history is determined per account and comparable regime, including reconciled older statements. Calibration depends on held-out target windows, not the requested export range or the age of the oldest file.

## Result publication

An analysis result stores its resolved definition, input revision, metric and model versions, values, adjustments, coverage, and evidence manifest. Large results live in R2 with metadata in Postgres. Snapshot extraction ends before statistical computation starts.

A correction schedules refreshes for affected subjects and periods. A publication transaction checks the current revision under the owner lock before selecting a completed result for live views. A stale result can remain historical evidence but cannot replace the current result. Publishing derived results does not itself advance the financial revision or trigger another analysis cycle.
