Stage 4 introduces historical exploration. Stage 5 adds commitments, ordinary costs, and scenarios. Stage 7 adds findings and intentions. Financial calculations are shared by the API, UI, and analyst; [Calculation rules](/technical/calculation-rules) defines their arithmetic.

## Historical comparisons

A query identifies its measure, currency, accounts, dates, date basis, and filters. Related totals and contributors use one consistent database read. Include supporting credits and counterparts even when their posting dates fall outside the displayed spending period.

Category, merchant, and account groups partition costs. Tags and personal events can overlap and are labelled accordingly. Contribution rows show the amount used by the calculation separately from the bank posting. A drill-down runs the same query and returns its current headline with the rows, so new data cannot silently mix with an old displayed total.

Frequency and average-payment comparisons use purchase counts and exact amounts. Higher average payments alone do not establish price inflation. Missing coverage remains separate from an observed decline.

## Saved analyses

Save a name and query definition, including fixed or relative date choices. Calculate when the view opens or the user presses refresh. Resolve relative dates using the current request time and selected timezone. Fixed dates remain fixed.

TanStack Query invalidates visible results after relevant writes. Closed saved analyses require no work. There is no automatic refresh queue, midnight scheduler, or permanent result history.

Saved analyses retain definitions only. Replayable historical reports are outside the current stages.

## Ordinary costs and commitments

Show actual costs separately from the estimate of an ordinary month. Use a recent mean with explicit exceptional-event exclusions, then add confirmed obligations without counting their historical payments twice. Show the selected weeks and the schedule contributions.

Recurring-pattern detection proposes schedules. The user confirms amount, cadence, dates, and account. Accepted schedules and payment matches can be corrected through the UI. Missing activity does not automatically cancel a commitment.

## Forecasts and scenarios

Start with a deterministic recent spending baseline, expected income, confirmed schedules, and explicit adjustments over 30 or 90 days. Show the assumptions and missing evidence. These are estimates, not probability intervals.

The cash calendar shows explicit scheduled deposit movements. Card purchases count as costs and settlements count as cash movements, once in their respective measures. Do not derive future cash balances from cost totals or silently simulate card cycles and loan interest. The [forecast contract](/technical/calculation-rules#forecast-contract) defines the supported outlook.

A scenario changes a copy of the baseline assumptions. Savings from reduced spending are described as estimated cost changes. Any substitution, such as more grocery spending after less delivery, is another explicit adjustment. Neither scenario edits actual history.

## Change detection and insight ranking

Stage 7 runs bounded deterministic checks on current history and confirmed schedules. Rank findings by material amount and persistence, with clear supporting periods. Stable finding identity and retained user state prevent unchanged scans from creating new notifications.

Opening Insights can run the current scan. A scheduled scan may do the same operation for in-app follow-up. A failed scan is visible and can be retried. It does not require dependency invalidation, versioned snapshots, or a generalized refresh coordinator.

Intentions compare a fixed baseline with a current period and any explicitly selected related costs. Results describe observed change without claiming causality. Missing post-change history produces an explanation rather than a guessed outcome.
