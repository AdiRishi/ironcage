## Accounts

Each account has a label and a kind: deposit, card, or loan. Bank identifiers appear once a file has established them. An offset relationship between a deposit account and the home loan can be recorded with the dates it applies.

## Categories

The spending and income trees, two levels deep. Rename, move, add, or archive a category. A category keeps its identity through renames and moves, so history stays comparable. When the model finds a group of transactions that fits no category well, it proposes a new subcategory here. You approve or dismiss it.

## Rules

A rule applies a category or role to transactions that match a condition, such as a counterparty, an account, or text in the description. It previews the transactions it would change and lists the exceptions you set by hand. Most of what rules used to do is now done by counterparty defaults, so rules are for the cases a counterparty cannot express, such as "transfers to this account on the 1st are rent".

## AI

Whether the model runs, which model it is, and exactly what it receives: descriptor text and category names, never amounts, balances, dates, or account numbers. Usage and cost so far. A confidence threshold decides which assignments apply automatically and which become questions.

## Display

Timezone, defaulting to Australia/Sydney, and reporting currency, defaulting to AUD.

## Data

Export, source files, and a statement of where backups and original files are kept.
