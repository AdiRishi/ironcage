## Accounts

Each account has a label, a bank, and a kind: deposit, card, or loan. Bank identifiers appear once a file has established them. An offset relationship between a deposit account and a loan can be recorded with the dates it applies. A label period names an account's transactions for a stretch of dates, such as "joint account while renting".

## Categories

The spending and income trees, two levels deep, with tags and personal events on the same page. Rename, move, add, or archive a category. A category keeps its identity through renames and moves, so history stays comparable. When the model finds a group of transactions that fits no category well, it proposes a new subcategory in [Questions](/product/questions), where you add it or dismiss it.

## Rules

A rule applies a category or role to transactions that match its conditions: an account, a role, a counterparty, a payment channel, and text in the description. It previews the transactions it would change and lists the exceptions you set by hand. Most of what rules used to do is now done by counterparty defaults, so rules are for the cases a counterparty cannot express, such as "transfers from the joint account whose description contains Rent are housing". When two rules match one transaction and disagree, it becomes a question.

## AI

Two things call a model, and each is off until you turn it on.

Counterparty identification names who each descriptor is. Settings says which model it is and exactly what it receives: descriptor text, payment channels, whether money went out or came in, account kinds, transaction counts, category names, existing counterparty names, and your own earlier answers as examples. Never amounts, balances, dates, or account numbers. A confidence threshold decides which answers apply automatically and which become questions. A button starts a run over every descriptor without a counterparty, and the recent runs are listed with how many names they identified. Another checks the model against your answers: it asks about every counterparty you set, without showing the model your answer, and shows how often the name and category agreed and how often answers at each confidence were right.

The [analyst](/product/analyst) answers questions in plain language, and once it is on, writes a briefing of each month after the month ends without being asked. Its switch reads "Answer questions and write monthly briefings with the model". Settings says which model it is and what it sends for each question and each month's briefing: the figures, dates, bank descriptions, and counterparty, category, tag, personal event, and account labels it works from, and the names of your files and any problems importing them. Never account numbers or balances. While it is off, the overview shows no briefing, and the analyst screen lists none and says to turn it on here in place of the question box. Your conversations and briefings are kept, and briefings show again once you turn it back on.

Model usage shows the calls, tokens, and recorded cost so far, in total and for each task: identification, checks against your answers, analyst questions, and monthly briefings. It counts the calls whose usage report was incomplete, and shows a figure that no call reported as unknown, not zero. One warning amount in US dollars covers them all. Once the recorded cost of every task together reaches it, nothing calls the model until you raise it.
