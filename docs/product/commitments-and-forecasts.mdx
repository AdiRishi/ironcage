Everything here is stage 5.

## Recurring payments

The app suggests recurring payments from history and shows the payments it found. The user confirms the amount, how often, which account pays, the next date, and when the schedule starts. Mortgage, strata, insurance, and subscriptions each get their own schedule.

A quarterly levy is paid quarterly in the cash calendar and spread across its three months in the ordinary-cost view. Changing an amount or the paying account from a date keeps the old amount for earlier dates. Each expected payment shows which actual payment matched it; the user can match, unmatch, or skip. A missed payment does not cancel a schedule.

## Cost outlook

Choose 30 or 90 days. The outlook shows the recent weeks it is based on, the variable estimate from them, each scheduled cost, any one-offs the user adds, expected income, and the projected surplus, all labelled as estimates. A scheduled payment appears once: its past occurrences are taken out of the variable estimate.

If there are not enough covered weeks, the estimate says so and the schedule list still works. The app never fills a gap in bank history with made-up activity.

## Cash calendar

Dated movements on the deposit accounts for the next 90 days, including card payments and loan repayments. These are movements, not costs; the card purchases were already costs when they happened. The latest known balance of each account is shown with its date. The app does not predict future balances, interest, or card cycles.

## Scenarios

Start from the current outlook and change things: less on delivery, more on groceries, a higher insurance premium from March, a one-off in June. Save it. The app shows the difference against the unchanged outlook as an estimated change in costs, income, or credits. History and confirmed schedules stay as they are.

## Example

Strata is $600 a quarter, paid on the first of July. August's ordinary cost carries about $198 of it, spread by days. The cash calendar shows $600 on 1 October and nothing in August. Cutting delivery by $200 a month while adding $80 to groceries shows as $360 less over 90 days.
