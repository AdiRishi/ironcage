The transaction list combines the supplied accounts while preserving the account behind each movement. Filters cover dates, amounts, accounts, merchants, categories, personal purposes, and review status.

A record detail shows the bank description and booked amount, its dates, the current interpretation, linked movements, and source evidence. Search works with both original descriptions and recognized merchant names.

## Money counted once

The examples below describe different views of the same records. They are fictional.

| Records                                                            | Spending view         | Account view                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| $100 grocery purchase on a card, followed by a $100 card repayment | $100 groceries        | Purchase increases card debt. Repayment reduces cash and card debt.      |
| $2,000 moved between two owned accounts                            | No purchase           | Both account movements remain visible.                                   |
| $300 dinner with $200 of confirmed reimbursements                  | $100 personal cost    | The original payment and incoming repayments remain visible.             |
| $120 purchase with a linked $40 refund                             | $80 net purchase cost | Both the payment and refund retain their bank dates.                     |
| $3,900 mortgage payment containing $2,800 interest                 | $2,800 interest cost  | $3,900 cash payment and $1,100 principal reduction are shown separately. |

The app never treats every transfer as internal or every incoming payment as income. A transfer that pays rent is still an expense. A loan drawdown is borrowing.

## Classification with several useful dimensions

A transaction has a primary financial role and can carry additional context. A dinner can be dining, social, part of a trip, and partly reimbursed. These descriptions answer different questions without duplicating the money.

Merchant views unite aliases for the same business. Distinct services remain distinguishable, such as rides and food delivery from the same provider. The original wording remains available.

Ordinary retail payments do not establish the exact items bought. The app can use an invoice, receipt, or Adi's explanation as additional evidence. It does not invent basket contents from a merchant name.

## Corrections and splits

Adi can change a category, merchant, purpose, linked refund, or transfer interpretation. A mixed purchase can be split into parts whose amounts equal the original total.

**Apply to this transaction** is the default. **Apply a rule** previews the matching records and distinguishes historical changes from future automatic classification. A one-off exception remains an exception when a general rule changes.

The change history records what changed. Undo restores the prior interpretation through a new correction. It does not erase the original bank evidence.

## Dates and currencies

The account view follows the bank posting date. Spending trends use the purchase date when the source reliably identifies it, otherwise the posting date. The selected basis is visible. A bank value date is not automatically described as the purchase date.

Foreign purchases show the booked Australian-dollar amount, any supplied original-currency amount, and associated fees. The app uses the actual booked amount for historical totals. An inferred fee relationship is identified as an inference until established.

## Unresolved relationships

When a refund or reimbursement arrives, the app proposes a relationship to the original expense. It supports partial repayments and several repayments against one expense.

An unresolved incoming transfer is not silently treated as salary. An ambiguous duplicate is not silently removed. Relevant views identify unresolved amounts and explain how they affect the answer.

The calculation rules are defined in [Financial records and metrics](/technical/financial-model).
