---
title: Counterparties
description: The businesses, people, and accounts on the other side of your money.
---

A counterparty is whoever is on the other side of a transaction: a business such as Woolworths, a person such as your landlord, one of your own accounts, or an institution such as the ATO or an insurer.

## The list

Counterparties for the selected period, largest outflow first, with inflows in their own tab. Each row shows the name, kind, default category, amount, and number of transactions. A search box matches names and the bank's descriptor text.

## A counterparty

The page for one counterparty shows:

- its name, kind, parent brand, default category, and provenance
- the descriptor texts that resolve to it, such as `WOOLWORTHS 1234 SYDNEY` and `WOOLWORTHS METRO 5678`
- a monthly chart of what you paid or received across the whole history
- its transactions

Changing the default category applies it to every transaction from this counterparty that you have not set by hand. Merging two counterparties moves every descriptor and transaction to one of them. Moving a descriptor fixes a misidentification, for example a descriptor that belongs to a different business with a similar name.

## People

Payments to people come in through bank transfers, such as "Transfer To Jane Smith". Ironcage cannot know who Jane is, so the first payment to a new person becomes a question: is this rent, a bill split, a gift, a loan, or money to yourself somewhere else? The answer becomes the person's default, so you answer once. When one person is paid for different things, such as rent and a share of a dinner, the reference on each transfer tells them apart: each reference can have its own answer, listed under By reference on the person's page, and anything without one follows the person's default.
