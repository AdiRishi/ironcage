Trends is the main place to investigate spending over time. Adi can compare weeks, months, quarters, years, rolling periods, or custom ranges. The same comparison can be broken down by category, merchant, account, purpose, or event.

## An investigation can keep going

A comparison begins with a difference and remains connected as Adi narrows the question.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the change">
    A monthly comparison shows which categories contributed to a spending increase.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect a contributor">
    Opening food separates groceries, dining, and delivery, with the same periods selected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand the pattern">
    Delivery shows order count, average payment, and total cost. The comparison explains which
    changed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the wider effect">
    Including groceries shows whether reduced delivery was offset by more shopping. The analyst can
    discuss the selected evidence.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each contributor links to records. The contributions add up to the displayed difference, including an explicit remainder when the view groups small contributors.

## Comparable periods

A partial month is labelled as partial. Its default comparison covers the same elapsed calendar dates in the comparison month. Adi can choose full periods or a daily average explicitly.

The app does not present a decrease as a saving when an account's data is missing. Changing from a debit account to a credit card preserves the combined purchase trend.

Percentage changes accompany absolute changes. A percentage is unavailable when the prior value is zero. The app shows the new amount instead. Small samples are identified rather than presented as strong patterns.

## Actual spending and ordinary costs

Actual spending includes exceptional purchases. The ordinary-cost view separately estimates the ongoing cost of normal life. It spreads known annual or quarterly obligations across the periods they cover and identifies excluded events.

Adi can inspect and change those adjustments. A trip remains visible in actual spending even when excluded from the ordinary-cost view. Repeated exceptional purchases are visible as a pattern of their own.

The app can compare periods before and after an event, such as the mortgage beginning. The event gives context. Timing alone does not prove that the event caused every change.

## Supported questions

The explorer and analyst support questions such as these:

- Where did my money go during this period?
- Which merchants or habits account for the increase?
- Am I buying more often, or paying more per transaction?
- What did a trip cost, including bookings paid months earlier?
- Has my ordinary monthly cost increased, after accounting for unusual expenses?
- Which commitments are new, and which costs have disappeared?
- Did a change I made produce a lasting difference?

A higher average transaction does not establish a price increase. Different products, quantities, or group sizes can change the average. Item-level claims require item-level evidence.

## Saved analyses

Adi can save the filters, comparison, adjustments, and question as a named analysis. New imports refresh the analysis with the same definition. A correction refreshes affected results.

Each completed result retains its data version and assumptions. Historical answers remain inspectable, while the current view identifies when a newer result is available.
