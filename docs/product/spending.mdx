title: Spending
description: Where money went, broken down as far as the evidence allows, and how it changes over time.

## The breakdown

Spending opens on the top-level categories for the selected period, largest first, each with its amount, its share, and a small line of the previous twelve months. Selecting a category opens its subcategories, then its counterparties, then the transactions. The path so far stays visible as a breadcrumb, so Food, then Dining out, then a specific restaurant is three selections away.

Every level shows the same four facts:

- the amount for the period
- the change against the comparison period, as an amount and a percentage, or "new" when the earlier period was zero
- how many purchases, and the average purchase
- how much of the amount rests on the model's judgment and not on you, a rule, or the bank

## Comparing periods

The comparison period defaults to the previous period of the same length. A month in progress compares with the same number of days of the previous month. You can choose the same period last year or any custom range.

A change splits into two parts: more purchases and dearer purchases. "Delivery rose $100: $45 from two more orders and $55 from a higher average order" means 10 orders at $20 became 12 orders at $25.

## Over time

A monthly chart shows any category, or all spending, across the history. Months with missing coverage are drawn differently and are not read as zero.

## Mixed retailers

Counterparties that sell a bit of everything, such as Amazon, Kmart, or Big W, land in Shopping, then General. Splitting a single purchase into categories is always available from the ledger.

## Saved analyses

Any spending view can be saved with a name. Opening it runs it against current records. A relative period, such as the last three months, moves with the calendar. A fixed period stays fixed.

## Examples

- "What did I spend on clothing this year?" Shopping, then Clothing, with the year selected.
- "Did groceries go up or did I just shop more?" Food, then Groceries, compared with last year. The purchases part and the average part answer it.
- "What does the car cost me?" Transport, with fuel, parking, tolls, servicing, insurance, and registration as subcategories.
