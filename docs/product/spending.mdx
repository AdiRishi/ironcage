## The breakdown

Spending opens on the top-level categories for the selected period, largest first, each with its amount, its share, and a small line of the previous twelve months. Selecting a category opens its subcategories, then its counterparties, then the transactions. The path so far stays visible as a breadcrumb, so Food, then Dining out, then a specific restaurant is three selections away. The address holds the period and the open category, so a bookmark reopens the same view.

Every level shows the same four facts:

- the amount for the period
- the change against the comparison period, as an amount and a percentage, or "new" when the earlier period was zero
- how many purchases, and the average purchase
- how much of the amount rests on the model's judgment and not on you, a rule, or the bank

## Comparing periods

A line under the title says what the period is compared with, such as "Compared with August 2026". For a month in progress it reads "25 days so far, compared with the same days of August 2026", and March in progress on the 30th compares with all of February. Selecting the comparison offers:

- the period before, with the same number of months, which is the default
- the same period last year
- chosen dates, picked on a calendar with both days included

Compare applies the choice. It stays in the address and carries over to the overview, so both screens compare the same dates. Every change names the dates it is compared with: "$310 more than in August 2025, which was $1,200". For a period in progress those are the same days, so 2026 on 25 September reads "$310 more than in 1 January to 25 September 2025, which was $1,200".

Days without records are not zero spending. When an account has no records for part of the comparison period, a line names the account and the dates, and the comparison leaves those days out. When the comparison period has no records at all, the page says there are no records to compare with, and each row shows "No records" in place of a change.

A change splits into two parts: more purchases and dearer purchases. "Delivery rose $100: $45 from two more orders and $55 from a higher average order" means 10 orders at $20 became 12 orders at $25.

## Over time

A chart beside the total shows the open category, or all spending, over the twelve months that end with the selected period, and each row has the same twelve months as a sparkline. The period strip shows all spending across the whole history. Months with missing coverage are drawn as an outline and are not read as zero.

## Mixed retailers

Counterparties that sell a bit of everything, such as Amazon, Kmart, or Big W, land in Shopping, then General. Splitting a single purchase into categories is always available from the ledger.

## Examples

- "What did I spend on clothing this year?" Shopping, then Clothing, with the year selected.
- "Did groceries go up or did I just shop more?" Food, then Groceries, compared with last year. The purchases part and the average part answer it.
- "What does the car cost me?" Transport, with fuel, parking, tolls, servicing, insurance, and registration as subcategories.
