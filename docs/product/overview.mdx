title: Overview
description: What happened to your money in a period, and what changed.
---

The overview is the home screen. It reads the selected period, which is the current month by default.

## Headline

Two figures lead the page: what came in and what went out. Money moved between your own accounts is in neither. A month in progress is labelled with the number of days elapsed. If an account's records stop partway through the period, the headline names the account and the missing dates.

## Flow

A flow diagram sits below the headline. Inflows enter from the left, grouped by source: salary, interest, refunds, and reimbursements. Outflows leave to the right, grouped by destination:

- spending, split into top-level categories
- loan repayments, split into principal and interest
- savings and investments held outside the four accounts
- anything not yet interpreted, shown as its own stream

The streams are proportional. Selecting a stream opens it: a spending category opens [Spending](/product/spending) at that category, and any other stream opens the [Ledger](/product/ledger) filtered to its records.

Surplus is inflow minus outflow. It is shown beside the flow, not as available cash.

## What changed

Compared with the previous period of the same length, a short list names the largest changes in plain sentences. Each sentence has an amount and a link to its records.

> Dining out rose $310 to $720. Eleven visits instead of six, and the average bill went from $58 to $65.

> Electricity was $0 this month and $284 last month. The quarterly bill has not arrived yet.

The sentences come from deterministic comparisons. The analyst can rewrite them into a briefing, but it never changes the numbers.

## Needs your eye

If there are open questions, a count with the amount at stake links to [Questions](/product/questions), for example "6 questions affect $1,420 this month". Files that failed to import appear here too.

## Coverage

A thin strip per account shows which days in the period have records and whether they reconcile. A gap is a gap: no records for those dates, not zero spending.

## First use

Before any import, the overview explains which files to download from NetBank, and how, and links to [Sources](/product/sources) to upload them.
