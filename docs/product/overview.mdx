The overview is the home screen. It reads the selected period, which is the current month by default.

## Headline

Three figures lead the page: what came in, what went out, and what was left over, or the shortfall when more went out than came in. Money moved between your own accounts is in none of them; a line under the flow says how much moved. Under what went out, a line says how much of the spending rests on the model's judgement. Under the title, a line says what the figures are compared with, and selecting it chooses another comparison, as on [Spending](/product/spending#comparing-periods). A month in progress is labelled with the number of days elapsed. If an account's records stop partway through the period, the headline says so, and if the comparison period lacks records, a line names the account and the missing dates. Each figure names the dates it is compared with, such as "$380 more than in August 2026", or says there are no records to compare with. For a period in progress those are only the same days, so September on the 25th reads "$380 more than in 1 to 25 August 2026".

## Flow

A flow diagram sits below the headline. Inflows enter from the left, grouped by source:

- income, split by top-level income category, such as salary and interest, with anything not yet categorised as its own stream
- money borrowed
- money from your accounts outside Ironcage
- anything not yet understood, as "Not yet understood, incoming"

Outflows leave to the right, grouped by destination:

- spending, split into top-level categories, with anything not yet categorised as its own stream
- loan principal: repayments less the interest already counted as spending
- money sent to your accounts outside Ironcage, such as savings and investments
- anything not yet understood, as "Not yet understood, outgoing"

Refunds and reimbursements are not inflows. They reduce the spending they return, so a $300 dinner with $200 repaid is $100 of dining. Loan interest is spending in its category, not part of a loan stream.

When more came in than went out, a "Kept" stream on the right balances the two sides. When more went out, a "From savings" stream on the left does. Either one equals the left-over figure or the shortfall in the headline. They have no records of their own, so they cannot be selected, and each says what it is: what came in less what went out, or what went out beyond what came in.

A spending category where more came back than went out, such as a travel refund that arrives a month after the trip, is drawn on the left as "Travel, net money back" with the amount returned. What went out in the headline is already net of it, so the line above the diagram names it apart from the sources: "$9,000 came in from 2 sources, and $5,260 went out to 4 destinations after $300 in Travel came back."

A button switches the diagram to a table of the same streams, grouped into what came in and what went out, and on a phone the streams are stacked bars.

The streams are proportional. Selecting a stream opens the records behind it: a spending category opens [Spending](/product/spending) at that category, and any other stream opens the [Ledger](/product/ledger#opened-from-a-number) at the transactions it counts, with a total equal to the stream. Every stream except Kept and From savings can be selected in the diagram, the table, and the bars, by pointer or keyboard. Each one is named with its direction and amount, such as "Not yet categorised, $120 went out", so income and spending not yet categorised stay apart.

The left-over figure is inflow minus outflow. It is not available cash.

## What changed

Compared with the comparison period chosen under the title, the period before unless you choose another, a short list names the largest changes in plain sentences. Its heading names the dates compared, such as "What changed compared with August 2026". Each sentence has an amount and a link to its records. When the comparison period has no records, the list says there is nothing to compare with instead of calling every category new.

> Dining out rose $310 to $720. Eleven visits instead of six, and the average bill went from $58 to $65.

> Electricity was $0 this month and $284 last month. The quarterly bill has not arrived yet.

The sentences come from deterministic comparisons. The analyst can rewrite them into a briefing, but it never changes the numbers.

## Needs your eye

If there are open questions, a count with the amount at stake links to [Questions](/product/questions), for example "6 questions affect $1,420 this month". Files that failed to import appear here too.

## Coverage

A thin strip per account shows which days in the period have records and whether they reconcile. A gap is a gap: no records for those dates, not zero spending.

## First use

Before any import, the overview explains which files to download from NetBank, and how, and links to [Sources](/product/sources) to upload them.
