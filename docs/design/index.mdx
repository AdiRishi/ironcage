## Brief

Ironcage is a private instrument for reading one person's money. You use it for years, often for a few minutes at a time, to answer a specific question. Its job is to make the answer the first thing you see and the evidence one selection away.

The visual material comes from the subject itself: Australian polymer banknotes. Their substrate is cool and mineral, their inks are deep and saturated, and their numerals are engraved wide and confident. Ironcage takes those three things and leaves everything else out. There is no card grid, no gradient, and no decoration that does not carry information.

## Palette

| Name     | Hex       | Use                                                                       |
| -------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Polymer  | `#EDF0EB` | Page ground. Cool and mineral, like the note substrate.                   |
| Sheet    | `#F9FAF7` | Surfaces that hold an object you work on: the ledger, a detail, a dialog. |
| Intaglio | `#16323A` | Text, primary strokes, primary actions. A deep blue-green ink.            |
| Slate    | `#5A6A6E` | Secondary text and quiet marks.                                           |
| Rule     | `#D2D9D1` | Dividers and control borders.                                             |
| Eucalypt | `#2E7D6B` | Inflow.                                                                   |
| Wattle   | `#C58F14` | Outflow.                                                                  |
| Waratah  | `#B0364A` | Something needs you: a question, a failure, a destructive action.         |

Spending is not judged. Outflow is gold, not red, and a rise in a category is never colored as bad. Waratah appears only when you need to act.

Categories take their hues from a categorical palette of eight, validated in this order so that neighbours stay distinct for colour-blind readers and each hue holds 3:1 contrast on Polymer and Sheet. Stacks and flows use the same order.

| Order | Category      | Hue       |
| ----- | ------------- | --------- |
| 1     | Housing       | `#5B4BAF` |
| 2     | Food          | `#C8402F` |
| 3     | Transport     | `#2F6FCF` |
| 4     | Shopping      | `#C9681A` |
| 5     | Bills         | `#1A86AD` |
| 6     | Health        | `#3E8A2E` |
| 7     | Entertainment | `#C0467F` |
| 8     | Travel        | `#8A6D00` |

Every other top-level category shares the neutral `#8C9A96` and is told apart by its label. Each top-level category keeps its hue everywhere: in the flow, in charts, and in its swatch. Subcategories use their parent's hue. The hues identify a category; they never judge it, so Food's red says nothing about how much was spent.

## Type

One family: **Archivo**, a grotesque with a width axis from 62 to 125. Width carries the hierarchy, so the page needs no second typeface.

| Role      | Width | Weight | Size / line | Notes                                                                   |
| --------- | ----- | ------ | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Figure    | 125   | 560    | 56 / 60     | The period's headline numbers. Tabular, lining figures.                 |
| Figure S  | 118   | 540    | 28 / 32     | Secondary numbers, such as a category total.                            |
| Title     | 112   | 640    | 24 / 30     | Page titles, such as "August 2026".                                     |
| Heading   | 106   | 620    | 18 / 24     | Section headings.                                                       |
| Body      | 100   | 420    | 15 / 24     | Prose, controls, and table rows.                                        |
| Small     | 100   | 440    | 13 / 18     | Secondary facts, in Slate.                                              |
| Condensed | 72    | 600    | 13 / 16     | Dense labels inside charts, such as month initials on the period strip. |

All figures use tabular lining numerals so columns of money align. Prose lines stay under 72 characters. Labels are sentence case. Nothing is set in all caps, and there is no monospace type.

## Layout

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ironcage   Overview  Spending  Ledger  Counterparties  Sources  Analyst   Questions 6   Upload │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ▂▃▅▂▃▆▃▂▄▅▃▂▃▅▂▃▆▃▂▄▅▃▂▃ ▇  period strip: one bar per month, scrolls     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ August 2026                                                            │
│ $8,500 in       $6,380 out        surplus $2,120                       │
│                                                                        │
│  Salary ════════╗                ╔══════ Housing                       │
│  Interest ══════╬════════════════╬══════ Food                          │
│  Repaid ════════╝                ╠══════ Transport                     │
│                                  ╚══════ Loan principal                │
│ ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │
│ What changed                          │ Needs your eye                  │
│ Dining out rose $310 to $720 ...      │ 6 questions affect $1,420       │
│                                       │ Coverage  ▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬ ▬▬        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- A top bar replaces the side rail, so the flow and the ledger get the full width.
- The period strip sits under the top bar on the overview, spending, ledger, and counterparties, the screens that read a period. Each month is a short bar of its outflow, or of its spending on Spending, and the selected period is inked in Intaglio. It is both the period control and a year-at-a-glance chart.
- Every chart of months draws them the same way. A month without records is a short dashed outline, never a zero bar. A month with some records missing is an outline at the height its records reach, because its amount is a lower bound.
- Content is left aligned on a 12-column grid, with a maximum width of 1280 pixels. Numbers in tables align right.
- Sections are separated by space and, at most, one Rule line. Surfaces are reserved for objects you work on. A section is never boxed just to look like a section.
- On a phone or a tablet, the top bar condenses to the wordmark, Questions, and a menu. On a phone, the period strip scrolls horizontally, and the flow becomes two stacked bars, in and out, because a flow diagram is unreadable at that width.

## Provenance marks

Every assignment shows who made it, with a small mark beside the value:

- a solid Intaglio dot for you or a rule
- a hollow Slate ring for the model
- a Waratah ring for an open question

Hovering or focusing the mark shows the reason and confidence. Totals show the model's share as a short phrase, such as "$410 of this is model-assigned".

## Motion

One orchestrated moment: when the overview loads, the flow streams draw from left to right over 600 milliseconds. Changing the period crossfades the figures. Opening a detail slides its sheet in from the right. Nothing else moves by itself. Reduced-motion settings replace all of these with instant changes.

## Writing

- Name things by what they are to you: Questions, not review items. Counterparties, not merchants or entities.
- Buttons say what happens: "Apply to all Woolworths transactions", not "Submit".
- Numbers carry their period: "$720 on dining out in August", not "$720".
- Errors say what happened and what to do, without apologizing: "The file is not a CommBank export. Upload a CSV, OFX, or PDF statement from NetBank."
- Empty screens invite the next action: "No records for March yet. Upload the March statements."

## Quality floor

Every flow works at 390 pixels wide and by keyboard alone. Focus is always visible as a 2 pixel Intaglio ring with a 2 pixel offset. Color never carries meaning alone: inflow and outflow also differ by position and sign, and provenance marks differ by shape. Text contrast meets WCAG AA on Polymer and Sheet. Every chart has a table view.
