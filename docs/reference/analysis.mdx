The API computes every number. SQL sums ledger facts and postings, and functions in `packages/finance/src/` do the rest of the arithmetic. No model computes a result. Narrating results is the planned analyst's job. Money examples are invented.

## Events and allocations

An accepted posting records a signed balance change. A financial event groups the postings of one economic event, has a nonnegative magnitude, and divides it into allocations.

| Event role                | Posting membership                              | Allocation role and reporting account                                    |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `purchase`                | One debit.                                      | `purchase`, optionally with a non-personal portion. The charged account. |
| `income`                  | One credit.                                     | `income`. The receiving account.                                         |
| `transfer`                | One or two movements between owned endpoints.   | `transfer`. The sending account when present.                            |
| `cardSettlement`          | Deposit debit, card credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The deposit account when present.                            |
| `borrowing`               | Loan debit, deposit credit, or the linked pair. | `borrowing`. The loan.                                                   |
| `loanPayment`             | Deposit debit, loan credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The loan.                                                    |
| `refund`, `reimbursement` | One credit.                                     | `refund` or `reimbursement`, with a category. The receiving account.     |
| `financingCost`           | One interest or fee debit.                      | `financingCost`, with a category. The charged account.                   |
| `unresolved`              | One posting whose role is unknown.              | `unresolved`. The posting's account.                                     |

Magnitude is the absolute amount of the primary posting. A linked pair counts the amount moved once.

## Measures

These measures come from ledger facts. [Preview impact](#preview-impact) uses a separate set with different definitions.

| Measure            | Result                         | Definition                                                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Spending           | Flow, spending, monthly series | Purchase and financing-cost allocations, minus refund and reimbursement allocations. Non-personal portions are excluded. |
| Income             | Flow                           | Income allocations. Excludes borrowing, transfers, refunds, and reimbursements.                                          |
| Inflow             | Flow, monthly series           | The sum of the flow's in streams.                                                                                        |
| Outflow            | Flow, monthly series           | The sum of the flow's out streams.                                                                                       |
| Internal           | Flow                           | Movements between ledger accounts. Reported as a total and never drawn.                                                  |
| Left over          | Overview                       | Inflow minus outflow. The overview says "Short by" when it is negative.                                                  |
| Unresolved outflow | Flow                           | Unresolved debits, shown as the out stream "Not yet understood".                                                         |
| Loan principal     | Flow                           | Loan repayments minus the interest and fees charged on loan accounts in the same period, never below zero.               |
| Purchase count     | Spending, flow changes         | Distinct purchase events with positive spending, including fully refunded ones.                                          |
| Model share        | Flow, spending                 | The part of spending whose category or role came from a counterparty the model set.                                      |

A refund or reimbursement reduces spending in its own category on its own date. When a credit link applies it to a specific purchase, the linked amount reduces spending in the purchase's category and period instead. Unlinked credits without a category reduce nothing and are listed as unresolved inflow.

Every flow and spending result names its period, comparison period, date basis, currency, and calculation time. The flow also reports coverage for each account: observed dates, reconciled intervals, missing periods, and the latest import.

## Ledger facts

The flow, the monthly series, and spending read a projection, not the event tables. Previews and the counterparty screens read events directly, as described in [Reads outside the projection](#reads-outside-the-projection). `ledger_facts` holds one row per allocation per effect, computed by `eventLedgerFacts` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/facts.ts`:

| Measure                         | Rows                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `spending`                      | Purchases and financing costs, positive. Refunds and reimbursements, negative. Non-personal parts are left out. |
| `income`                        | Income allocations.                                                                                             |
| `internal`                      | Transfers between ledger accounts, card settlements, and the loan side of a repayment.                          |
| `externalOut`, `externalIn`     | Transfers to and from a counterparty of kind `ownAccount`.                                                      |
| `loanRepayment`                 | A repayment, counted once from the account the cash left.                                                       |
| `borrowing`                     | A drawdown, counted once from the loan side.                                                                    |
| `unresolvedOut`, `unresolvedIn` | Unresolved events by direction, and credits with no category or link.                                           |

Each row carries its account, counterparty, category and top-level category, posted and spending dates, amount, whether it is a purchase, and whether the value that placed it came from a model-set counterparty.

A credit linked to a purchase is emitted by the purchase's event, as a negative `spending` row in the purchase's category, dates, account, and counterparty. The credit's own event emits only the part that no link applies. So the facts of an event depend only on its own rows and the credit links that touch it, and a change to the purchase moves the linked reduction with it. A credit linked to a non-personal part of a purchase reduces nothing, because that part is not spending.

Statement triggers note which events a transaction changed. Any change to an event, its allocations, its posting membership, a credit link, a counterparty's kind or source, or a category's parent notes the affected events. A credit link notes both of its events. So does a change to a posting's posted date, value date, account, currency, or amount, for the events that contain it. The notes live in a transaction-local setting, `ironcage.fact_events`, written by `note_fact_events` in migration `0022_serializable_writes.sql`, so concurrent transactions never read or write a shared marker. `writeTransaction` in `workers/api/src/database/transactions.ts` runs `refreshNotedFacts` after the write's work and before it commits. Every write to those tables runs through it, so a committed write leaves no fact behind. Period queries sum facts over indexes on `(currency, measure, spending_on)` and `(currency, measure, posted_on)`.

### Rebuilding after a derivation change

`factsVersion` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/facts.ts` names the derivation, and every event records the version that built its facts. A deploy that changes `eventLedgerFacts` bumps the version. Commands refresh only the events they change, so older facts are rebuilt in the background instead of inside someone's command. Reading `getFactsStatus` counts events with older facts and keeps a `FactsWorkflow` running in the processor until none are left. The Workflow calls `rebuildFactsBatch`, which rebuilds 500 events per transaction. `fact_rebuilds` records the instance for each version, and a status read starts a new instance when the last one ended with facts left. While a rebuild runs, the web app shows how many transactions are left, and it refetches every screen when the rebuild finishes.

A test in `workers/api/tests/analysis/facts.test.ts` runs a sequence of commands, then rebuilds every event from scratch and requires the same rows. It protects the rule that no change can leave a fact behind.

A refresh recomputes facts from the event as it is stored. That leaves one known gap. A reviewed correction that changes a posting's amount or currency rewrites the posting but not the event's magnitude or allocations, which are set only when the event is created. The refreshed facts keep the old allocation amounts, so the flow and spending disagree with the posting. No command recomputes the magnitude, and a correction's allocations must still sum to the old magnitude.

## Flow

`summarizeFlow` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/flow.ts` turns summed facts into streams:

| Side | Streams                                                                                                                             |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| In   | Income by income category, borrowing, transfers from your other accounts, and credits not yet understood.                           |
| Out  | Spending by top-level category in taxonomy order, loan principal, transfers to your other accounts, and outflow not yet understood. |

Loan principal is repayments minus the interest and fees charged on loan accounts in the same period, never below zero. Interest is already spending, so cash out equals spending plus principal. Movements between ledger accounts are reported as a total and never drawn.

The overview's list of changes is the six subcategories whose spending changed most, each split into a purchases part and an average part.

`getMonthlyFlow` in `workers/api/src/analysis/flows.ts` returns inflow, outflow, and spending for every calendar month from the first month with a ledger fact through the current month, by spending date. It runs `summarizeFlow` once per month. Each month carries a coverage mark. A month is `complete` when every account whose records span it has no missing days in it, `partial` when some account has observed days in it, and `missing` otherwise. The period strip, spending, and Sources read the series, and a screen without `?period=` uses it to find the latest month with records.

## Comparisons

The flow and spending operations take `{ period, comparison, basis, currency }` and sum ledger facts for both periods in one query. Spending also takes a category, and a null category means all spending. Its rows are the selected category's direct children, or the top-level categories when none is selected. Each row also carries a 12-month series that ends with the month containing the period's last day. The result lists the 25 counterparties with the most spending in the period within the selected category.

### Periods

Periods are `[start, endExclusive)`. Relative selections support rolling day counts and calendar weeks, months, quarters, and years. Weeks start on Monday. A relative selection resolves once per request in the settings timezone. The comparison is the previous period, the same period a year earlier, or fixed dates. A period in progress compares with the same number of elapsed days of the comparison period, clamped at month end. Fixed dates never move. Neither alignment fills unobserved days with zero; missing coverage is reported.

### Changes

Absolute change is current minus previous. Relative change is the absolute change over previous, only when previous is nonzero. A zero previous gives no percentage, and the UI says "new".

The purchases part of a change is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2` and the average part is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`, where `n` is the purchase count and `p` the average purchase. The two sum exactly to the change. Intermediate values are exact rationals. `purchaseDecomposition` floors each part to cents and gives the remaining cent to the part with the larger remainder, so the parts sum to the change.

Example: 10 delivery orders totalling $200 in July, 12 totalling $300 in August. The change is $100. The purchases part is `(12 - 10) * (20 + 25) / 2 = $45`, and the average part is `(25 - 20) * (10 + 12) / 2 = $55`.

## Reads outside the projection

### Preview impact

Correction, rule, and relationship previews compute their impact from events and allocations, not from `ledger_facts`. `previewPeriod` in `workers/api/src/events/impact.ts` works one calendar month at a time. It reads every active event whose primary posting falls in the month, swaps in the proposed events, and runs `periodMeasures` from `packages/finance/src/impact.ts` before and after. A preview uses the posted date and every account in the currency. A change whose postings span several months returns one impact per month.

| Measure                 | Definition                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gross costs             | Purchase and financing-cost allocations, including non-personal portions.                                                                                           |
| Net personal costs      | Gross costs of personal allocations minus the credits linked to them.                                                                                               |
| Income                  | Income allocations.                                                                                                                                                 |
| Cash balance change     | The sum of deposit-account postings in the month. Absent unless every deposit account has reconciled coverage for the whole month.                                  |
| Loan repayments         | Loan payments reported on a loan account.                                                                                                                           |
| Loan interest and fees  | Financing costs reported on a loan account.                                                                                                                         |
| Net principal reduction | Loan repayments minus loan interest and fees. Absent unless every loan account has reconciled coverage for the month and no unresolved event is reported on a loan. |

These definitions differ from the flow's. Net personal costs subtracts only linked credits, while Spending also subtracts unlinked refunds and reimbursements that have a category. Net principal reduction has no floor, so interest charged before a repayment makes it negative, while the flow's loan principal stops at zero. Previews always use the posted date, and the screens use the spending date. A preview's figures can therefore disagree with the flow for the same month.

### Counterparty totals

The counterparty list and detail read events and their primary postings directly, through `summaries` in `workers/api/src/interpretation/counterparties.ts`. Outflow and inflow are the booked amounts of each active event's primary posting, split by sign. The list limits them to the selected period by posted date. The detail's totals and its monthly history cover all records. These totals include transfers, non-personal portions, and unresolved events, and a refund counts as inflow instead of reducing spending. A counterparty's amounts can therefore differ from its spending on the Spending screen.
