The API computes every number. SQL sums ledger facts and postings, and functions in `packages/finance/src/` do the rest of the arithmetic. No model computes a result. Narrating results is the planned analyst's job. Money examples are invented.

## Events and allocations

An accepted posting records a signed balance change. A financial event groups the postings of one economic event, has a nonnegative magnitude, and divides it into allocations.

| Event role                | Posting membership                              | Allocation role and reporting account                                    |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `purchase`                | One debit.                                      | `purchase`, optionally with a non-personal portion. The charged account. |
| `income`                  | One credit.                                     | `income`. The receiving account.                                         |
| `transfer`                | One or two movements between owned endpoints.   | `transfer`. The sending account when present.                            |
| `cardSettlement`          | Deposit debit, card credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The deposit account when present.                            |
| `borrowing`               | Loan debit, deposit credit, or the linked pair. | `borrowing`. The loan.                                                   |
| `loanPayment`             | Deposit debit, loan credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The loan.                                                    |
| `refund`, `reimbursement` | One credit.                                     | `refund` or `reimbursement`, with a category. The receiving account.     |
| `financingCost`           | One interest or fee debit.                      | `financingCost`, with a category. The charged account.                   |
| `unresolved`              | One posting whose role is unknown.              | `unresolved`. The posting's account.                                     |

Magnitude is the absolute amount of the primary posting. A linked pair counts the amount moved once.

## Measures

These measures come from ledger facts, and every screen, preview, and counterparty total reads them the same way.

| Measure            | Result                         | Definition                                                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Spending           | Flow, spending, monthly series | Purchase and financing-cost allocations, minus refund and reimbursement allocations. Non-personal portions are excluded. |
| Income             | Flow                           | Income allocations. Excludes borrowing, transfers, refunds, and reimbursements.                                          |
| Inflow             | Flow, monthly series           | The sum of the flow's in streams.                                                                                        |
| Outflow            | Flow, monthly series           | The sum of the flow's out streams.                                                                                       |
| Internal           | Flow                           | Movements between ledger accounts. Reported as a total and never drawn.                                                  |
| Left over          | Overview                       | Inflow minus outflow. The overview says "Short by" when it is negative.                                                  |
| Unresolved outflow | Flow                           | Unresolved debits, shown as the out stream "Not yet understood".                                                         |
| Loan principal     | Flow                           | Loan repayments minus the interest and fees charged on loan accounts in the same period, never below zero.               |
| Purchase count     | Spending, flow changes         | Distinct purchase events with positive spending, including fully refunded ones.                                          |
| Model share        | Flow, spending                 | The part of spending whose category or role came from a counterparty the model set.                                      |

A refund or reimbursement reduces spending in its own category on its own date. When a credit link applies it to a specific purchase, the linked amount reduces spending in the purchase's category and period instead. Unlinked credits without a category reduce nothing and are listed as unresolved inflow.

Every flow and spending result names its period, comparison period, date basis, currency, and calculation time, and says whether the comparison period has records (see [Periods](#periods)). The flow also reports coverage for each account: observed dates, reconciled intervals, missing periods, and the latest import.

## Ledger facts

The flow, the monthly series, spending, previews, and counterparty totals read a projection, not the event tables. `ledger_facts` holds one row per allocation per effect, computed by `eventLedgerFacts` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/facts.ts`:

| Measure                         | Rows                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `spending`                      | Purchases and financing costs, positive. Refunds and reimbursements, negative. Non-personal parts are left out. |
| `income`                        | Income allocations.                                                                                             |
| `internal`                      | Transfers between ledger accounts, card settlements, and the loan side of a repayment.                          |
| `externalOut`, `externalIn`     | Transfers to and from a counterparty of kind `ownAccount`.                                                      |
| `loanRepayment`                 | A repayment, counted once from the account the cash left.                                                       |
| `borrowing`                     | A drawdown, counted once from the loan side.                                                                    |
| `unresolvedOut`, `unresolvedIn` | Unresolved events by direction, and credits with no category or link.                                           |

Each row carries its account, counterparty, category and top-level category, posted and spending dates, amount, whether it is a purchase, and whether the value that placed it came from a model-set counterparty.

A credit linked to a purchase is emitted by the purchase's event, as a negative `spending` row in the purchase's category, dates, account, and counterparty. The credit's own event emits only the part that no link applies. So the facts of an event depend only on its own rows and the credit links that touch it, and a change to the purchase moves the linked reduction with it. A credit linked to a non-personal part of a purchase reduces nothing, because that part is not spending.

Statement triggers note which events a transaction changed. Any change to an event, its allocations, its posting membership, a credit link, a counterparty's kind or source, or a category's parent notes the affected events. A credit link notes both of its events. So does a change to a posting's posted date, value date, account, currency, or amount, for the events that contain it. The notes live in a transaction-local setting, `ironcage.fact_events`, written by `note_fact_events` in migration `0022_serializable_writes.sql`, so concurrent transactions never read or write a shared marker. `writeTransaction` in `workers/api/src/database/transactions.ts` runs `refreshNotedFacts` after the write's work and before it commits. Every write to those tables runs through it, so a committed write leaves no fact behind. Period queries sum facts over indexes on `(currency, measure, spending_on)` and `(currency, measure, posted_on)`.

### Rebuilding after a derivation change

`factsVersion` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/facts.ts` names the derivation, and every event records the version that built its facts. A deploy that changes `eventLedgerFacts` bumps the version. Commands refresh only the events they change, so older facts are rebuilt in the background instead of inside someone's command. Reading `getFactsStatus` counts events with older facts and keeps a `FactsWorkflow` running in the processor until none are left. The Workflow calls `rebuildFactsBatch`, which rebuilds 500 events per transaction. `fact_rebuilds` records the instance for each version, and a status read starts a new instance when the last one ended with facts left. While a rebuild runs, the web app shows how many transactions are left, and it refetches every screen when the rebuild finishes.

A test in `workers/api/tests/analysis/facts.test.ts` runs a sequence of commands, then rebuilds every event from scratch and requires the same rows. It protects the rule that no change can leave a fact behind.

A reviewed correction that changes a posting's amount or currency changes its event too. `syncEventAmounts` in `workers/api/src/interpretation/engine.ts` runs in the same transaction. An event with one posting and one allocation takes the new magnitude and allocation amount. An event that is split, linked to a credit, or joined to another posting by a movement cannot be divided again without you, so the correction fails with `conflict` until you undo that change.

## Flow

`summarizeFlow` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/flow.ts` turns summed facts into streams:

| Side | Streams                                                                                                                             |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| In   | Income by income category, borrowing, transfers from your other accounts, and credits not yet understood.                           |
| Out  | Spending by top-level category in taxonomy order, loan principal, transfers to your other accounts, and outflow not yet understood. |

Loan principal is repayments minus the interest and fees charged on loan accounts in the same period, never below zero. Interest is already spending, so cash out equals spending plus principal. Movements between ledger accounts are reported as a total and never drawn.

The overview's list of changes is the six subcategories whose spending changed most, each split into a purchases part and an average part.

`getMonthlyFlow` in `workers/api/src/analysis/flows.ts` returns inflow, outflow, and spending for every calendar month from the first month with a ledger fact through the month containing today in the settings timezone, by spending date. It runs `summarizeFlow` once per month. Each month carries a coverage mark. A month is `complete` when every account whose records span it has no missing days in it, `partial` when some account has observed days in it, and `missing` otherwise. The period strip, spending, and Sources read the series, and a screen without `?period=` uses it to find the latest month with records.

## Comparisons

The flow and spending operations take `{ period, comparison, basis, currency }` and sum ledger facts for both periods in one query. Spending also takes a category, and a null category means all spending. Its rows are the selected category's direct children, or the top-level categories when none is selected. Each row also carries a 12-month series that ends with the month containing the period's last day, from `trailingYear` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/periods.ts`. A period in year 0001 has no twelve months behind it, so spending fails with `invalid`. The result lists the 25 counterparties with the most spending in the period within the selected category.

### Periods

Periods are `[start, endExclusive)`. Every read that takes a selection resolves it once per request through `resolvePeriods` in `workers/api/src/analysis/periods.ts`. It reads today in the settings timezone inside the read's transaction and passes it to `resolvePeriod` and `comparisonPeriod` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/periods.ts`:

| Selection  | Period                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `months`   | Whole calendar months, `from` through `to`, written `YYYY-MM`. The month containing today ends after today. Earlier and later months resolve whole. The last month is before December 9999, whose period would end in year 10000. |
| `calendar` | Weeks, months, quarters, or years counted back from the one containing today. Weeks start on Monday.                                                                                                                              |
| `rolling`  | A number of days ending today.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `fixed`    | The given dates.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

`comparisonPeriod` moves the period back. `previous` moves it by its own span: a number of months for `months` and calendar months, quarters, and years, and a number of days for weeks, rolling, and fixed periods. `previousYear` moves it back twelve months. A `fixed` comparison is the given dates.

A whole period made of months compares with whole months, so February 2029 compares with all of February 2028, 29 February included. Any other period, including a month in progress, ends the day after its last day moved back. Month arithmetic clamps that day to the end of a shorter month, so a comparison never runs past the end of its own months. On 25 September 2026, September so far compares with 1 to 25 August 2026, or with 1 to 25 September 2025 for the year before. July to September 2026 compares with 1 April to 25 June 2026. On 30 March 2027, March compares with all of February 2027 and nothing more. On 25 September 2028, 2028 compares with 1 January to 25 September 2027, the same calendar days rather than the same number of days.

Neither period fills days without records with zero. `comparisonCoverage` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/coverage.ts` counts a day as covered for an account when it falls between the first and last record of one of the account's files, or inside a reconciled statement. A reconciled statement proves that its days without transactions had none. It looks for gaps only in the accounts that cover part of the period, since an account with no records in the period cannot make the comparison read as zero. For each one, the gaps are the comparison's days the account does not cover. The comparison is `missing` when no account covers any of it, `partial` when some account has a gap, and `complete` otherwise. So a card opened this month, compared with last month that the everyday account covers, is `partial` with the card's gap, not `missing`.

`periodLabel` and `monthLabel` in `packages/finance/src/analysis/labels.ts` name periods in Australian English: "August 2026", "2026", "March to May 2026", "November 2025 to February 2026", "1 to 25 August 2026", "3 March to 14 April 2026", and "3 March 2026".

### Changes

Absolute change is current minus previous. Relative change is the absolute change over previous, only when previous is nonzero. A zero previous gives no percentage. When the comparison period has records, Spending shows "New" and the overview says "none in" the comparison period. When `comparisonCoverage` is `missing`, both screens say there are no records to compare with (see [Periods](#periods)).

The purchases part of a change is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2` and the average part is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`, where `n` is the purchase count and `p` the average purchase. The two sum exactly to the change. Intermediate values are exact rationals. `purchaseDecomposition` floors each part to cents and gives the remaining cent to the part with the larger remainder, so the parts sum to the change.

Example: 10 delivery orders totalling $200 in July, 12 totalling $300 in August. The change is $100. The purchases part is `(12 - 10) * (20 + 25) / 2 = $45`, and the average part is `(25 - 20) * (10 + 12) / 2 = $55`.

## Previews

Correction, rule, and relationship previews show what a change does to the flow of every month it touches, by spending date. `previewImpacts` in `workers/api/src/analysis/preview.ts` derives the facts of the events as the change leaves them, and of the events whose credit links it adds or removes. It collects the months of their stored and new facts. For each month it runs `summarizePeriod` from `workers/api/src/analysis/flows.ts`, the same query and `summarizeFlow` call the overview runs. It then writes the new facts inside a savepoint, summarises each month again, and rolls the savepoint back. A preview therefore shows each month exactly as the overview will show it once the change is saved.

Each month reports what came in, what went out, spending, income, loan principal, both "not yet understood" streams, money moved between your accounts, and how much of spending rests on the model.

## Counterparty totals

The counterparty list and detail sum ledger facts by counterparty, through `summaries` in `workers/api/src/interpretation/counterparties.ts`. A counterparty's outflow is its share of the out streams: `spending`, `externalOut`, `loanRepayment`, and `unresolvedOut` facts, so a refund reduces it. Its inflow is its share of the in streams: `income`, `borrowing`, `externalIn`, and `unresolvedIn`. Movements between your own accounts count in neither. The list limits both to the selected period by spending date, and the detail's monthly history covers all records. A counterparty's outflow in a category equals its row on the Spending screen. A credit linked to a purchase reduces the purchase's counterparty, not the one who paid it back.
