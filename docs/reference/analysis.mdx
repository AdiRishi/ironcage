The API computes every number with functions in `packages/finance/src/`. The model narrates results and never computes them. Money examples are invented.

## Events and allocations

An accepted posting records a signed balance change. A financial event groups the postings of one economic event, has a nonnegative magnitude, and divides it into allocations.

| Event role                | Posting membership                              | Allocation role and reporting account                                    |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `purchase`                | One debit.                                      | `purchase`, optionally with a non-personal portion. The charged account. |
| `income`                  | One credit.                                     | `income`. The receiving account.                                         |
| `transfer`                | One or two movements between owned endpoints.   | `transfer`. The sending account when present.                            |
| `cardSettlement`          | Deposit debit, card credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The deposit account when present.                            |
| `borrowing`               | Loan debit, deposit credit, or the linked pair. | `borrowing`. The loan.                                                   |
| `loanPayment`             | Deposit debit, loan credit, or the linked pair. | `transfer`. The loan.                                                    |
| `refund`, `reimbursement` | One credit.                                     | `refund` or `reimbursement`, with a category. The receiving account.     |
| `financingCost`           | One interest or fee debit.                      | `financingCost`, with a category. The charged account.                   |
| `unresolved`              | One posting whose role is unknown.              | `unresolved`. The posting's account.                                     |

Magnitude is the absolute amount of the primary posting. A linked pair counts the amount moved once.

## Measures

| Measure                 | Definition                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Spending                | Purchase and financing-cost allocations, minus refund and reimbursement allocations. Non-personal portions are excluded. |
| Gross spending          | Purchase and financing-cost allocations before any credit.                                                               |
| Income                  | Income allocations. Excludes borrowing, transfers, refunds, and reimbursements.                                          |
| Surplus                 | Income minus spending.                                                                                                   |
| Unresolved outflow      | Unresolved debits. Shown beside every total they could change.                                                           |
| Cash balance change     | Closing minus opening balance across selected deposit accounts. Requires both anchors.                                   |
| Net principal reduction | Loan-side repayments minus interest and fees charged in the period. Requires full loan coverage of the period.           |
| Purchase count          | Distinct purchase events, including fully refunded ones.                                                                 |
| Model share             | The part of a total whose category or role came from a counterparty the model set.                                       |

A refund or reimbursement reduces spending in its own category on its own date. When a credit link applies it to a specific purchase, the linked amount reduces spending in the purchase's category and period instead. Unlinked credits without a category reduce nothing and are listed as unresolved inflow.

Every result names its measure, period, date basis, accounts, currency, calculation time, and coverage. Coverage reports observed dates, reconciled intervals, missing accounts or periods, and the count and amount of unresolved events.

## Flow

The flow of a period partitions every allocation on the selected accounts into exactly one stream:

| Side    | Stream                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| In      | Income by income category. Refunds and reimbursements are netted into the spending category they belong to.                                            |
| Out     | Spending by top-level category. Loan principal (`loanPayment` minus interest). Transfers to external own accounts and investments. Unresolved outflow. |
| Neither | Transfers, card settlements, and loan payments between accounts in the ledger. Reported as a count and amount, never drawn as flow.                    |

Total in minus total out equals surplus plus external transfers. The flow never shows a stream it cannot account for; unresolved money has its own stream.

## Ledger facts

Analysis reads a projection, not the event tables. `ledger_facts` has one row per allocation per effect:

| Column                           | Meaning                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `allocation_id`, `event_id`      | The source allocation.                                                                  |
| `account_id`                     | The reporting account.                                                                  |
| `posted_on`, `spending_on`       | The date on each basis. A credit-linked amount carries its purchase's dates.            |
| `measure`                        | `spending`, `income`, `transfer`, `loanPrincipal`, `externalTransfer`, or `unresolved`. |
| `category_id`, `top_category_id` | The category and its top-level ancestor.                                                |
| `counterparty_id`                | The event's counterparty.                                                               |
| `amount_minor`, `currency`       | The signed contribution to the measure. Credits are negative spending.                  |
| `model_assigned`                 | Whether the value that placed this row came from a model-set counterparty.              |

The command transaction that changes an event, allocation, credit link, counterparty default, or category rewrites that event's facts before commit. `packages/finance` owns the function that turns an event into facts, so the projection and every measure share one definition. A query sums facts for its period with indexes on `(measure, spending_on)` and `(measure, posted_on)`. It never loads history outside its period.

A migration that changes the fact function rebuilds the table from events in one transaction.

## Comparisons

A query is `{ measure, period, comparison, basis, currency, accounts, filters }`. Filters within one dimension combine with OR, dimensions with AND, and a category includes its descendants.

### Periods

Periods are `[start, endExclusive)`. Relative selections support rolling day counts and calendar weeks, months, quarters, and years. Weeks start on Monday. A relative selection resolves once per request in the settings timezone. A period in progress compares with the same number of elapsed days of the comparison period, clamped at month end. Fixed dates never move. Neither alignment fills unobserved days with zero; missing coverage is reported.

### Deltas and contributors

Absolute change is current minus previous. Relative change is the absolute change over previous, only when previous is nonzero. A zero previous gives no percentage, and the UI says "new".

Contributors sort by absolute change descending, then by group key. Category, counterparty, and account partition the total, so the view shows ten rows and a remainder. Tags and personal events overlap, so their rows show no remainder.

The purchases part of a change is `(n1 - n0) * (p0 + p1) / 2` and the average part is `(p1 - p0) * (n0 + n1) / 2`, where `n` is the purchase count and `p` the average purchase. The two sum exactly to the change. Intermediate values are exact rationals. Rounded parts use largest-remainder allocation with a stable key, so they sum to the rounded change: floor each part to cents, then give the remaining cents to the parts with the largest remainders.

Example: 10 delivery orders totalling $200 in July, 12 totalling $300 in August. The change is $100. The purchases part is `(12 - 10) * (20 + 25) / 2 = $45`, and the average part is `(25 - 20) * (10 + 12) / 2 = $55`.

### Daily average

A daily average divides a period's total by its calendar days after alignment. Missing coverage anywhere in the period makes the average unavailable, while the total and the day count stay visible.

## Saved analyses

A saved analysis stores a name and a query. Opening it runs the query against current records. Relative dates resolve at that time, and fixed dates stay fixed. Results are not stored.
